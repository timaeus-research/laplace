## 1. Recommended route: use the vertex section, then separate an affine space from a finite hull

I would choose **(b)**. You have already proved the difficult combinatorial statement identifying the minimal face. There is no need to reconstruct it by supporting-cut descent.

The missing geometric ingredient should be packaged as:

> **A nonempty face of a finite hull, itself generated by some of the generators, is exposed.**

The clean separation is **not** between the face and the remaining hull. It is between the **affine span of the face** and the remaining hull. That forces the separating functional to be constant on the face.

Below, all Lean statements are schematic; I am not asserting unverified Mathlib theorem names.

### Step 1: recover relative interior from your absorption lemma

Put
\[
T=\{v\in V:a_v(M)>0\},\qquad F=\operatorname{conv}T.
\]
Your existing results give:

- \(T\ne\varnothing\);
- \(F\subseteq P\), \(M\in F\);
- \(F\) is extreme in \(P\);
- \(F\) is contained in every extreme subset of \(P\) containing \(M\);
- for every \(b\in F\), there are \(z\in F\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1\) such that
  \[
  M=\varepsilon b+(1-\varepsilon)z.
  \]

To obtain \(M\in\operatorname{ri}F\), use just two standard finite-dimensional convex facts:

1. A nonempty convex set has nonempty intrinsic interior.
2. If \(b\in\operatorname{ri}F\), \(z\in F\), and \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), then
   \(\varepsilon b+(1-\varepsilon)z\in\operatorname{ri}F\).

Choose an intrinsic-interior point \(b\), then apply absorption.

This avoids proving a special “strictly positive convex combination lies in intrinsic interior” theorem. Such a theorem would also work, but you already have the stronger absorption input.

### Step 2: affine saturation

Prove the reusable lemma
\[
P\cap\operatorname{aff}F=F.
\]

The proof is short. Fix \(b\in\operatorname{ri}F\). For \(y\in P\cap\operatorname{aff}F\), relative interior supplies a point
\[
z=b+\delta(b-y)\in F,\qquad \delta>0.
\]
Thus \(b\) lies strictly between \(y\) and \(z\). Extremality of \(F\) gives \(y\in F\).

Schematic statement:

```lean
lemma inter_affineSpan_eq_of_isExtreme
    (hP : Convex ℝ P)
    (hF : Convex ℝ F)
    (hFP : F ⊆ P)
    (hface : Set.IsExtreme ℝ P F)
    (hri : (intrinsicInterior ℝ F).Nonempty) :
    P ∩ (affineSpan ℝ F : Set E) = F
```

The precise extreme-set API may already incorporate some of these hypotheses.

**Fallback if intrinsic-interior API becomes expensive:** use your positive coefficients directly. For \(y\in\operatorname{aff}T\), choose signed affine coefficients \(\lambda_v\) for \(y\). Since every \(a_v(M)\) on \(T\) is positive, choose small \(\varepsilon>0\) with
\[
a_v(M)-\varepsilon\lambda_v\ge0.
\]
Then \(M=\varepsilon y+(1-\varepsilon)z\) for \(z\in F\). Extremality again gives affine saturation. This is finite algebra, with no supporting-hyperplane theorem.

### Step 3: separate the affine span from the remaining generators

First ensure that \(T\) is **saturated**:
\[
T=V\cap F.
\]
Your finite Csiszár equivalence supplies this directly. Alternatively, replace \(T\) by \(V\cap F\); its hull remains \(F\).

Let
\[
D=V\setminus T,\qquad K=\operatorname{conv}D.
\]

If \(D=\varnothing\), take \(u=0,\beta=0\): the exposed face is all of \(P\).

Otherwise,
\[
K\cap\operatorname{aff}F=\varnothing.
\]
Indeed, an intersection point would belong to \(F\) by affine saturation. A finite convex combination belonging to an extreme subset forces every positively weighted generator into that subset, contradicting \(D\cap F=\varnothing\).

This also corrects the separation concern in the question:

> Once \(T=V\cap F\) and \(F\) is a face, \(\operatorname{conv}T\) and \(\operatorname{conv}(V\setminus T)\) cannot intersect.

But separating those two compact sets alone does not force constancy on \(F\), hence the affine-span version.

Now apply finite-dimensional strong separation to:

- the closed affine space \(\operatorname{aff}F\);
- the compact convex set \(K\).

The separator is bounded on one side on the entire affine space, so it annihilates its direction space. Consequently, after orienting it,
\[
\ell(x)=\beta\quad(x\in\operatorname{aff}F),\qquad
\ell(v)<\beta\quad(v\in D).
\]

Thus
\[
\ell(v)\le\beta\quad(v\in V),\qquad
F=P\cap\{\ell=\beta\}.
\]

Your `convexHull_inter_hyperplane` finishes the identification after converting the continuous linear functional to inner-product form.

### The single hardest step to isolate

Package **Steps 2–3**, independently of entropy:

```lean
lemma exists_exposingFunctional_of_finiteHull_face
    (V T : Finset E)
    (hTV : T ⊆ V)
    (hT : T.Nonempty)
    (hface :
      Set.IsExtreme ℝ
        (convexHull ℝ (V : Set E))
        (convexHull ℝ (T : Set E))) :
    ∃ ℓ : StrongDual ℝ E, ∃ β : ℝ,
      (∀ v ∈ V, ℓ v ≤ β) ∧
      convexHull ℝ (T : Set E) =
        {x ∈ convexHull ℝ (V : Set E) | ℓ x = β}
```

For the intermediate strict-inequality statement, use the saturated generator set \(V\cap\operatorname{conv}T\), not an arbitrary input \(T\).

If compact-versus-closed strong separation is awkward in Mathlib, use an orthogonal quotient:

- choose \(b\in F\);
- let \(L\) be the direction space of \(\operatorname{aff}F\);
- project \(v-b\) onto \(L^\perp\);
- show \(0\notin\operatorname{conv}\{\pi(v-b):v\in D\}\);
- strictly separate this compact convex hull from \(0\).

That requires only point-versus-compact separation and makes constancy on \(F\) automatic.

I would not guess the current separation theorem names. The useful statement to search for is **strict separation of a compact convex set and a disjoint closed convex set**, not a supporting theorem phrased through `intrinsicInterior`.

### Final assembly

The entropy-specific theorem then becomes almost bookkeeping:

```lean
∃ u β F,
  (∀ v ∈ V, inner u v ≤ β) ∧
  F = {x ∈ P | inner u x = β} ∧
  M ∈ intrinsicInterior ℝ F ∧
  F = convexHull ℝ {v ∈ (V : Set E) | 0 < a M v} ∧
  ∀ G, Set.IsExtreme ℝ P G → M ∈ G → F ⊆ G
```

This route is preferable to (a): descent adds strict-cardinality decrease, extension of affine-space functionals, and finite-slack perturbation bookkeeping, although your finite simplex theorem has already identified the answer.

## 2. What option (c) can do—and where its proposed proof is circular

The conditional-mean assertion is **true for these faces**, but it is not something to assume while proving integral extremality. Showing that a law carried outside \(F\) cannot have mean in \(F\) is essentially the desired theorem again.

There is, however, a clean reduction to your existing `ExtremeMeanSupport`.

Using affine saturation, choose \(b\in F\), and let
\[
\pi(x)=\operatorname{proj}_{L^\perp}(x-b).
\]
Then
\[
P\cap\pi^{-1}(0)=F,
\]
and \(0\) is an extreme point of \(\pi(P)\). To check extremality, lift an open-segment relation at \(0\) to \(P\); its convex combination lies in \(F\), so both lifted endpoints belong to \(F\).

If a law has mean in \(F\), its projected mean is \(0\). Apply `ExtremeMeanSupport` to \(\pi\circ S\), obtaining
\[
\pi(S)=0\ \text{a.e.},\qquad S\in F\ \text{a.e.}
\]

So (c) is viable, and worth extracting as a reusable integral-extremality theorem. It avoids exposure **after affine saturation**, rather than avoiding all the geometry.

### Which downstream statements actually require an exposing vector?

- **Essential support**, finite-entropy projection comparisons, and **absolute-continuity face order** do not inherently require \(u\).
- Neither does **finite-\(p\) continuity**, once the uniform density bound and \(L^1\) continuity are available.
- An alternative proof of the restricted moment-body identity can use charged vertex fibres directly.
- **Natural rays**, their limiting identifications, gap functions, boundary-layer estimates, and Fisher-accessibility estimates genuinely use an exposing functional.

Thus avoiding exposure would bypass only part of the desired atlas.

For the bound, with
\[
m_*=\min_{v\in\operatorname{ext}P}\nu(S=v)>0,
\]
a facewise exponential density satisfies
\[
\frac{dq_M}{d\nu}\le m_*^{-1},
\]
because its exponential numerator attains its maximum on a vertex of that face, and the normalizer includes that vertex fibre. This gives \(L^p\) continuity for every finite \(p\) from \(L^1\) continuity. Do **not** advertise \(L^\infty\) continuity: it can fail near a continuously populated face.

## 3. What `ResponseAtlas` should contain

I would land a modest capstone now, then pursue genuinely new geometry.

### The capstone’s mathematical interface

For each nonempty face \(F\), define the face law and effective parameter space
\[
\nu_F=\nu(\,\cdot\mid S\in F),\qquad
L_F=\operatorname{span}(F-F).
\]
Use \(L_F\), or the equivalent quotient by annihilator directions, rather than redundant ambient parameters.

`ResponseAtlas` should organize:

1. **Canonical stratum assignment**
   \[
   M\longmapsto F_M,\qquad
   P=\bigsqcup_F\operatorname{ri}F.
   \]

2. **Facewise exponential representation**
   \[
   q_M=q^{\,F_M}_{\theta_{F_M}(M)}.
   \]

3. **Support and incidence**
   \[
   q_M\sim\nu|_{\{S\in F_M\}},\qquad
   q_M\ll q_N\iff F_M\subseteq F_N.
   \]

4. **Topological realization**
   The mean/projection correspondence identifies \(P\) with the completed family in \(L^1\), Hellinger, and each finite-\(p\) density topology under the uniform bound.

5. **Facewise differential structure**
   \[
   \nabla\log Z_F:\Theta_F\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{ri}F,
   \qquad
   D^2\log Z_F=\operatorname{Cov}_{q_\theta^F}(S),
   \]
   with covariance positive definite on \(L_F\), including the zero-dimensional convention.

6. **Incidence-compatible boundary limits**
   Exposing rays connect containing strata to their subfaces; rate assumptions remain explicit.

I would make this a thin organizational layer, not one giant structure requiring every quantitative theorem as a field.

### Natural-parameter compactification: topology is the substance

Set-theoretically,
\[
\bigsqcup_F\Theta_F\longleftrightarrow P
\]
is immediate after the facewise atlas. It must **not** carry the coproduct topology.

Pulling back the topology of \(P\) defines a compactification, but alone is mostly repackaging. The substantive theorem would characterize convergence directly in natural parameters:

- normal divergence selecting a face;
- convergence of effective tangential parameters;
- iterated scales selecting flags of faces.

A single fixed exposing ray does not describe every escaping parameter sequence.

Facewise CLTs are useful, but fixed-face CLTs are comparatively standard. CLTs **uniform near stratum changes**, with changing covariance rank, would add substantially more depth.

## 4. The deeper missing target: distinguish topological completion from Fisher completion

Your current results suggest a particularly valuable next problem:

> **Which boundary responses are at finite intrinsic Fisher distance?**

Polynomial boundary layers give sufficient conditions. Charged polytopes alone do not.

Here is a sharp warning example. Take \(S(x)=x\) on \([0,1]\), with
\[
\nu=p_0\delta_0+p_1\delta_1+
c\,\frac{\mathbf1_{(0,e^{-1})}(x)}
{x(\log(1/x))^2}\,dx,
\qquad p_0,p_1,c>0,\quad p_0+p_1+c=1.
\]
Both vertices are charged, so your compact mean lift applies.

Along \(q_t\propto e^{-tx}\nu\), the positive-gap boundary layer satisfies
\[
H(r)=\frac{c}{\log(1/r)}
\]
for small \(r\). Standard Laplace asymptotics give
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{q_t}(X)
\sim \frac{c}{p_0\,t^2(\log t)^2}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\int^\infty\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{q_t}(X)}\,dt
=\infty,
\]
although \(q_t\to\delta_0\) in \(L^1\) and Hellinger.

In this one-dimensional family, there is no alternative finite-length route to that endpoint. Thus the Fisher metric completion need not equal the compact response completion.

This yields a strong next research program:

- formalize the distinction with one counterexample;
- sharpen boundary-layer hypotheses toward Fisher-accessibility criteria;
- determine when Fisher distance induces the response topology up to faces;
- separate ambient Hellinger geometry from intrinsic Fisher length geometry.

**My ordering:** minimal-face exposure → thin `ResponseAtlas` → effective facewise Fisher/analytic charts → Fisher-boundary classification. Natural-parameter convergence by face flags is the other genuinely deep branch.
