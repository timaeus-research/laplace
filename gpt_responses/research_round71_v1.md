## 1. Top candidate: charged polytopes are exactly the TV-completable bodies

Assume throughout: finite-dimensional statistic space \(E\), bounded measurable \(S\), probability reference \(\nu\), and compact moment body \(P\).

### The characterization

The following are equivalent:

1. \(P\) is generated by finitely many charged statistic values:
   \[
   P=\operatorname{conv}V,\qquad \nu\{S=v\}>0\quad(v\in V).
   \]
2. There exists a continuous feasible section
   \[
   \sigma:P\longrightarrow \operatorname{probL1}(\nu),
   \qquad \operatorname{meanL1}(\sigma(M))=M.
   \]
3. There exists a compact set \(K\subseteq\operatorname{probL1}(\nu)\) whose mean image is all of \(P\).
4. The finite-rate entropy projections exist throughout \(P\) and their densities give a continuous section in \(L^1(\nu)\).

Your completion proves \(1\Rightarrow4\Rightarrow2\Rightarrow3\). The new theorem is \(3\Rightarrow1\).

Importantly, the necessity argument uses **no entropy minimization**.

### Why necessity holds

If \(e\) is an extreme point of \(P\), every feasible law with mean \(e\) satisfies
\[
\mu\{S=e\}=1.
\]

Consequently:

* existence of an absolutely continuous feasible law at \(e\) forces \(\nu\{S=e\}>0\);
* feasible probability densities over distinct extreme points have disjoint essential supports, hence \(L^1\)-distance exactly \(2\).

An infinite collection of extreme points would therefore force \(K\) to contain an infinite \(2\)-separated subset. That contradicts compactness.

Thus \(P\) has finitely many extreme points. Finite-dimensional compact convex geometry gives
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{ext}P),
\]
and those extreme points are charged.

This is the clean answer to “beyond polytopes”: **not with a TV-continuous feasible completion on the entire closed body.** One must weaken the topology, omit boundary points, or change the completion space.

### The single hardest lemma

**An extreme mean forces concentration on its statistic fibre.**

The safest route does not use exposedness or Straszewicz:

1. Suppose some coordinate \(S_j\) is not almost surely equal to \(e_j\) under the feasible law.
2. Split the law on \(\{S_j>e_j\}\) and its complement.
3. Both normalized restrictions have means in \(P\), using your ambient mean theorem.
4. Their nontrivial convex combination is \(e\).
5. Extremality forces both conditional means to equal \(e\).
6. The first conditional mean has \(j\)-coordinate strictly greater than \(e_j\), contradiction.

The remaining compactness argument is a finite-cover argument with balls of radius \(<1\). The other possible library bottleneck is the finite-dimensional compact-convex theorem identifying a body with the closed convex hull of its extreme points.

### Consequences for the proposed alternatives

* **“Every exposed point is charged” is insufficient.** Even “every extreme point is charged” is insufficient: take countably many points on a circle, including their accumulation point, and assign positive mass to every point. At the extreme means the projections are the normalized individual fibres, mutually at distance \(2\).
* A full-dimensional **strictly convex body in dimension at least two** has uncountably many extreme points. A finite measure cannot charge all corresponding disjoint fibres. A whole-body absolutely continuous completion is already impossible before continuity is considered.
* This promotes the earlier counterexample into a **classification theorem**, rather than another isolated pathology.

## 2. Second candidate: the face lattice is the support lattice

### Minimal first module: `PolyhedralFaceSupport`

For every nonempty face \(F\) of \(P\), every feasible probability law \(\mu\), and its mean \(M\),
\[
M\in F \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mu\{S\in F\}=1.
\]

Here the law must be supported on \(P\) through its statistic; this is automatic for laws absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\).

For a polytope, expose \(F\) by an affine slack \(g\ge0\) on \(P\), with \(F=P\cap\{g=0\}\). Then
\[
g(M)=\int g(S)\,d\mu,
\]
and a nonnegative integrand has integral zero exactly when it vanishes almost surely.

A useful Lean interface is:
```lean
theorem mean_mem_face_iff_ae_stat_mem
    (hF : IsFaceOf P F)
    (hμ : μ ≪ ν)
    [IsProbabilityMeasure μ] :
    mean S μ ∈ F ↔ ∀ᵐ x ∂μ, S x ∈ F
```
Adapt `IsFaceOf` to the face representation already available; an initial exposed-face version minimizes infrastructure.

This is the minimal first module: no Hessians, no new entropy analysis.

### Minimal-face support: what actually follows?

For \(M\in\operatorname{ri}F\),
\[
q_M \sim \nu\!\restriction\{S\in F\}.
\]
Equivalently, its density is zero off that fibre and strictly positive almost everywhere on it.

Call this **essential support**, not topological support on \(X\); the latter requires additional topological hypotheses.

Your domination lemma plus the vertex section **alone** does not prove positivity throughout the face: vertex witnesses only occupy vertex fibres. But there is a short repair avoiding identification of the existing exposing chain:

1. Let \(\nu_F\) be normalized restriction to \(\{S\in F\}\), with mean \(b_F\).
2. Since \(M\in\operatorname{ri}F\), choose small \(\varepsilon>0\) such that
   \[
   N=\frac{M-\varepsilon b_F}{1-\varepsilon}\in F.
   \]
3. Choose a charged-vertex witness \(r_N\) with mean \(N\).
4. Set
   \[
   r=\varepsilon\nu_F+(1-\varepsilon)r_N.
   \]
   It has bounded density, finite entropy, mean \(M\), and density strictly positive throughout the face fibre.
5. Domination gives \(r\ll q_M\), hence \(\nu_F\ll q_M\).
6. Face support gives the reverse absolute continuity.

So: **domination plus a full-face witness suffices**. The exposing functional supplies the clean face-support lemma; it need not identify the projection’s original chain.

### Uniform density bound

Let \(V=\operatorname{ext}P\), \(m_*=\min_{v\in V}\nu\{S=v\}>0\). Then
\[
0\le \frac{dq_M}{d\nu}\le m_*^{-1}
\qquad\text{a.e., for every }M\in P.
\]

On the minimal face, write the projection density as normalized \(e^\ell\). The affine functional \(\ell\) reaches its maximum at a vertex \(v_*\) of that face. The denominator is at least
\[
\nu\{S=v_*\}e^{\ell(v_*)},
\]
while the numerator is at most \(e^{\ell(v_*)}\).

This is substantially cleaner than carrying face-dependent constants.

### Stratified Fisher theorem

First prove
\[
\operatorname{momentBody}(\nu_F)=F.
\]

Upper inclusion comes from support. Lower inclusion comes from the charged vertices of \(F\), whose convex hull is \(F\).

Let \(V_F=\operatorname{span}(F-F)\). On \(\operatorname{ri}F\):
\[
Dq_M[h]
  =q_M\,\big\langle \operatorname{Cov}_{q_M}(S)|_{V_F}^{-1}h,\ S-M\big\rangle,
\]
and
\[
D^2\mathcal I_F(M)[h,k]
 =\big\langle h,\operatorname{Cov}_{q_M}(S)|_{V_F}^{-1}k\big\rangle.
\]

Seabed route: normalized face reference → intrinsic feature coordinates on \(V_F\) → existing analytic atlas and Fisher theorem → transport back to \(L^1(\nu)\). The rate differs from the face-reference rate only by \(-\log\nu\{S\in F\}\).

**Coordinate warning:** Fisher in natural coordinates is covariance; Fisher in mean coordinates, and the rate Hessian, is inverse covariance.

## 3. Natural-ray degeneration: exact, but not a response-ray identity

For any target \(M\in\operatorname{ri}F\), extend its facewise log-density affinely to the ambient space, writing \(w=e^\ell>0\). Choose an exposing slack \(g\).

Define
\[
A=\int_{\{g=0\}}w\,d\nu,\qquad
B_t=\int_{\{g>0\}}we^{-tg}\,d\nu.
\]
Then the ambient natural ray
\[
q_t=\frac{we^{-tg}}{A+B_t}\nu
\]
converges to \(q_M=A^{-1}w1_{\{g=0\}}\nu\), and
\[
\boxed{\ \|q_t-q_M\|_1=\frac{2B_t}{A+B_t}\ }.
\]

This gives **every completed boundary response an explicit natural-parameter approach**. Its mean curve need not be a straight ray.

For \(M_s=m_0+s(M-m_0)\), no comparable universal exact identity follows: the conditional law on the terminal face generally changes too. If \(\beta_s=q_{M_s}(S\notin F)\) and \(r_s=q_{M_s}(\cdot\mid S\in F)\), then
\[
2\beta_s\le\|q_{M_s}-q_M\|_1
\le2\beta_s+(1-\beta_s)\|r_s-q_M\|_1.
\]

Rates require boundary-layer information:

* \(g\ge\gamma>0\) off the face gives exponential convergence.
* \(\int_{0<g\le r}w\,d\nu\le Kr^\alpha\) gives \(B_t=O(t^{-\alpha})\).
* Charged vertices alone give neither an off-face gap nor a universal rate.

There is also **no uniform TV modulus depending only on \(P\) and \(m_*\)**. On \(P=[0,1]\), fix equal endpoint masses and place a fixed remaining mass at \(\varepsilon\). Tilt toward \(0\) with parameter of order \(\varepsilon^{-1}\). The resulting mean tends to \(0\), but its mass at \(\varepsilon\), hence its distance from the endpoint projection, stays bounded away from zero. The reference varies with \(\varepsilon\), while \(P,m_*\) remain fixed.

## 4. Capstone and reconstruction

### `ResponseAtlas`

Worth writing after the two leading projects, with the headline:

> **Charged polytopes are precisely the compact moment bodies admitting continuous density-valued mean sections; their entropy section is a compact homeomorphic, facewise analytic, mean-preserving deformation retract, with facewise Fisher geometry.**

Package the explicit natural-parameter Taylor radius separately from the facewise mean-coordinate atlas: they are different quantitative statements.

Also retain the semantic distinction: the endpoint is the **information projection of the data law**, not necessarily the data law itself. Equality requires the data law to belong to the completed family.

### Delta method and CLT

The deterministic \(L^1\) delta lemma should be a corollary of the analytic atlas **if that atlas already proves Fréchet analyticity into \(L^1\)**. One-dimensional Taylor control alone is not automatically that theorem.

On a face, for sampling law \(\mu\) with mean \(M\in\operatorname{ri}F\),
\[
\sqrt n\bigl(q_{\widehat M_n}-q_M\bigr)
\Rightarrow Dq_M[G],\qquad
G\sim N(0,\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(S)|_{V_F}).
\]
The limit is finite-rank and \(L^1\)-valued; it needs no general Banach-space CLT.

Coordinatewise CLTs alone do **not** prove joint convergence. Use a multivariate CLT or scalar CLTs for every linear combination plus Cramér–Wold. I cannot verify the declarations in your September-2026 Mathlib snapshot; inspect that dependency before committing a module plan.

**Bottom line:** first prove that your completed world is the maximal possible whole-body TV world. Then identify its faces as exact support strata and put the existing analytic/Fisher machinery on each stratum.
