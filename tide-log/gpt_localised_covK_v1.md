## 1. A and B: correct leading constants, with a few qualifications

**A’s constants are correct.** Independently substituting,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\lambda}{2}c_3'
+\frac{\alpha}{6}\frac3{\lambda^2}
-\frac12c
&=-\frac{5\alpha}{4\lambda^2}+\frac{3a}{2\lambda}
+\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda^2}
+\frac{\alpha}{4\lambda^2}-\frac{a}{2\lambda}\\
&=-\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda^2}+\frac a\lambda=c.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the square probe, the constant is
\[
\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac3{\lambda^2}
-\frac12\frac1\lambda=\frac1\lambda.
\]
The stated moment estimates therefore give both covariance estimates with error \(K/t\), for all sufficiently large \(t\).

**Both fifth- and sixth-moment boundedness claims are correct.** The fifth bound must mean
\[
\left|t^3\langle x^5\rangle_{\rm loc}\right|\le K;
\]
it is a signed-moment cancellation, not an \(O(t^{-3})\) bound on \(\langle |x|^5\rangle_{\rm loc}\).

There is a substantially cheaper fifth-moment proof than the proposed expansion through envelopes of degree fourteen. Write \(D=\langle\phi\rangle\). For fixed \(g>0\), \(\phi'\) is bounded, so
\[
|\phi(x)-1|\le C|x|.
\]
Consequently,
\[
|\langle x^5\phi\rangle|
\le |\langle x^5\rangle|+C\langle x^6\rangle=O(t^{-3}).
\]
Divide by \(D\ge 1/2\). For \(g=0\), \(a=gx_0=0\), so \(\phi=1\).

If bounded-derivative infrastructure is inconvenient in Lean, use
\[
|e^u-1|\le \max(1,e^M)|u|,\qquad
u=ax-\tfrac g2x^2\le M,
\]
and \(|x|^7\le (x^6+x^8)/2\). This only needs even-moment bounds of degrees six and eight. The sixth-moment bound follows directly from \(\phi\le e^M\) and \(D\ge1/2\).

**The localiser reading is right for fixed \(g,x_0\):** its leading contribution is \(ba/\lambda\); centred localisation does not change the leading constant. Precisely,
\[
-\partial_t\frac{a}{t\lambda+g}
=\frac{a\lambda}{(t\lambda+g)^2},
\qquad
t^2\frac{a\lambda}{(t\lambda+g)^2}\longrightarrow\frac a\lambda.
\]
Thus the identification is fair **at leading order**, not as an exact finite-\(t\) formula for the true mean.

Two concrete qualifications:

- The seabed summary’s “\(t\langle x^2\rangle_{\rm loc}\to1/\lambda\), \(K/t^2\)” cannot literally be the generic scaled error bound. Normally,
  \[
  |\langle x^2\rangle_{\rm loc}-1/(t\lambda)|\le K/t^2,
  \]
  hence the **scaled** error is \(K/t\). A nonzero second-order coefficient rules out the stronger reading.
- B’s frame coefficients are correct if \(b\) denotes the probe’s linear coefficient **about \(c\)**. For an origin-based probe \(\psi(w)=\frac12w^\top Bw+b^\top w\), with symmetric \(B\),
  \[
  \widetilde b=Q^\top(Bc+b),
  \]
  not just \(Q^\top b\). Check the precise convention in `probe_affineFrame`.

Subject to that convention and the stated integrability hypotheses, **B is correct**.

## 2. Infrastructure: use the two-family decomposition

**Yes: separating the measure family from the observable family is the right abstraction.** The localised measure is generated by `locPotential1`, but the energy observable is the original \(\ell_i\). This distinction should be visible in the generic lemma rather than hidden in an anharmonic special case.

The two proposed identities follow directly from product-measure independence. In particular, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left[\sum_k\varphi_k,u_i u_j\right]
=
\langle u_j\rangle\operatorname{Cov}_i[\varphi_i,x]
+\langle u_i\rangle\operatorname{Cov}_j[\varphi_j,x].
\]
The localized mean and covariance bounds then give the claimed \(O(1/t)\) bound after multiplying this expression by \(t^2\).

**The derivative identity is conceptually cleaner, but not automatically a cheaper asymptotic proof.** For a time-dependent potential \(U_t\) and probe \(\psi_t\), the general identity is
\[
\frac d{dt}\langle\psi_t\rangle_t
=
\langle\partial_t\psi_t\rangle_t
-\operatorname{Cov}_t\!\left[\psi_t,\ U_t+t\partial_tU_t\right].
\]
Here
\[
U_t=\ell+\frac{g}{2t}(x-x_0)^2,
\qquad U_t+t\partial_tU_t=\ell,
\]
so a fixed probe satisfies exactly
\[
-\partial_t\langle\psi\rangle_{\rm loc}
=\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm loc}[\ell,\psi].
\]

I cannot determine from the listed lemma names whether the seabed already supports this time-dependent version. A fixed-potential `hasDerivAt_gibbsExpectation` theorem does **not** justify inserting `locPotential1 t` and ignoring its derivative.

Main pitfalls:

- Differentiation under the integral needs suitable local domination.
- The covariance is with \(\ell\), not with \(U_t\).
- An \(O(t^{-2})\) mean remainder cannot simply be differentiated to obtain an \(O(t^{-3})\) covariance remainder.

For this tide, the algebraic moment route plus the two-family lemmas is the safer implementation.

## 3. C: right missing term, wrong “only if” wording

The diagnosed extra leading term is correct:
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm loc}[L,\psi]
=
\frac1{t^2}\left[
\text{unlocalised leading coefficient}
+b^\top gH^{-1}(w_0-w_*)
\right]+O(t^{-3}),
\]
with \(b=\nabla\psi(w_*)\).

But **“correct only for \(w_0=w_*\)” is too strong for a fixed probe**. Agreement also occurs when
\[
b^\top gH^{-1}(w_0-w_*)=0,
\]
including \(g=0\), \(b=0\), or an appropriate orthogonality. For fixed \(g>0\), agreement **for every linear probe** is equivalent to \(w_0=w_*\).

Recommended wording:

> Tide 63’s anchor-free `covKFormulaLoc` reproduces the exact localised covariance’s leading coefficient for centred localisation. For a general anchor, it omits the contribution \(b^\top gH^{-1}(w_0-w_*)/t^2\), except where that scalar vanishes. This contribution is the leading negative time derivative of the anchor displacement in the localised mean.

Against the note, distinguish the **displayed approximation**, its centring assumptions, and the **exact localised expectation**. If the displayed mean includes the anchor displacement, its negative derivative must include that displacement’s derivative. If it deliberately suppresses the anchor term, the formalisation should say so rather than call the anchor-free expression the derivative of the full displayed mean.

## 4. D: Stein is useful, but keep second order as a follow-up

The coefficients \(2c'\) and \(2c_2'\) are the expected ones. A justified differentiable expansion
\[
m(t)=\frac{c}{t}+\frac{c'}{t^2}+\cdots
\]
gives
\[
t^2[-m'(t)]=c+\frac{2c'}t+\cdots.
\]
The caveat is again that an ordinary remainder estimate alone does not justify this differentiation.

**The proposed Stein identity is exact and has straightforward analytic boundary conditions** for polynomial \(f\):
\[
\langle f'\rangle_{\rm loc}
=
\langle f(t\ell'+gx-a)\rangle_{\rm loc}.
\]
Quartic decay makes the boundary terms vanish. Writing \(m_k=\langle x^k\rangle_{\rm loc}\), it gives, for \(k\ge1\),
\[
(t\lambda+g)m_{k+1}
+\frac{t\alpha}{2}m_{k+2}
+\frac{t\gamma}{6}m_{k+3}
=k\,m_{k-1}+a\,m_k.
\]

This is useful infrastructure, but it does not close the hierarchy automatically. For example, it yields the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm loc}[\ell,x^k]
={}&\frac{k}{2t}m_k
-\frac{g}{2t}\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm loc}[x^2,x^k]
+\frac{a}{2t}\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm loc}[x,x^k]\\
&-\frac{\alpha}{12}\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm loc}[x^3,x^k]
-\frac{\gamma}{24}\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm loc}[x^4,x^k].
\end{aligned}
\]
Higher-moment asymptotics remain necessary.

For the direct route, second order requires second coefficients for \(m_3,m_4\), leading coefficients for \(m_5,m_6\), and controlled remainders, alongside the lower-moment expansions. A formally established Stein recurrence could reduce that work, but **it is not a free consequence of tides 72/73’s mean coefficients**.

I would keep D deferred unless the needed one-dimensional integration-by-parts machinery and higher-moment coefficients are already readily available.
