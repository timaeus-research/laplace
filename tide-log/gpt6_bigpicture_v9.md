### 2. R6: coefficient maps as bounded linear maps

The coefficient maps are linear in the amplitude coefficient array \(c\).

For a finite pole set \(F\), choose \(b<r<\rho\) and a weighted \(L^1\) norm that controls both ordinary and logarithmic moments, for example
\[
\|c\|_{r,F}
=
\sum_{i,j}r^{i+j}
\int_0^\infty
e^{-\beta s^2}
\left(\sum_{\alpha\in F}s^{\alpha-1}(1+|\log s|)\right)
|c_{ij}(s)|\,ds .
\]
Then prove
\[
\sum_{\alpha\in F}
\bigl(|A_\alpha(c)-A_\alpha(d)|
     +|B_\alpha(c)-B_\alpha(d)|\bigr)
\le K_F\|c-d\|_{r,F}.
\]

The finite-part series makes this a weighted sequence-functional estimate.

**Use \(r<\rho\), not the envelope radius \(\rho\) itself.** The pointwise bound \(C\rho^{-i-j}\) need not be summable after weighting by \(\rho^{i+j}\).

This is coefficient stability; continuity of a remainder estimate under the same norm is a separate assertion.

### 5. R8: general-\(d\) constant product density

The logarithmic-coordinate convolution formulation is the right first target.

For \(x_\ell\in(0,b)\), put
\[
t=\prod_{\ell=1}^d x_\ell^{k_\ell},\qquad
A=b^{\sum_\ell k_\ell},\qquad
p_\ell=(h_\ell+1)/k_\ell.
\]
Under \(r_\ell=k_\ell\log(b/x_\ell)\), the density becomes
\[
\frac{\prod_\ell b^{h_\ell+1}/k_\ell}{t}
\left(e^{-p_1\cdot}*\cdots*e^{-p_d\cdot}\right)
\!\left(\log(A/t)\right),
\qquad 0<t<A.

\[
\boxed{
\frac{t^{p-1}}{\prod_\ell k_\ell}
\frac{(\log(A/t))^{d-1}}{(d-1)!}.

\]
