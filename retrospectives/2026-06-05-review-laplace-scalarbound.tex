\begin{abstract}
A soundness review of \texttt{Laplace/ScalarBound.lean}: the elementary scalar Taylor-remainder
bounds on $e^{-z}$ that feed the integral-remainder machinery. The first-order bound is clean. The
second-order bound is the \textbf{first genuine statement-versus-claim mismatch of the laplace review
marathon}: the exported headline proves a constant $1/2$ where its own section intro --- and the true
Taylor remainder --- give the sharp $1/6$. Per the review-tide rule this is reported, not rewritten,
and escalated to the user. One dead \texttt{open} was landed alongside.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
The scalar substrate beneath \reviewref[IntegralRemainder]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-oned-integralremainder}:
it proves $|e^{-z}-(1-z)|\le(z^2/2)\max(1,e^{-z})$ (first order) and the second-order analogue, the
former consumed directly by the perturbative-remainder integrand estimate. A pure file of elementary
calculus inequalities --- no integrals.

\section{What was reviewed}
The first-order chain ($1-z\le e^{-z}$; nonnegativity; the $z\ge0$ and $z\le0$ case bounds; the
absolute headline \texttt{abs\_exp\_neg\_sub\_one\_add\_le}) and the second-order chain
(the $z\ge0$ and $z\le0$ branches and the absolute headline
\texttt{abs\_exp\_neg\_sub\_one\_add\_sub\_half\_sq\_le}), checked constant-by-constant against the
Taylor remainders.

\section{Fidelity / soundness findings}
\textbf{First order: clean.} Every constant and case bound matches: $z\ge0$ gives $z^2/2$, $z\le0$
gives $(z^2/2)e^{-z}$, and the headline assembles them with the $\max(1,e^{-z})$ factor and the
correct $1/2$.

\textbf{Second order: the exported constant is loose by a factor of three.} The $z\le0$ branch
\texttt{exp\_neg\_sub\_one\_add\_sub\_half\_sq\_le\_half\_cube\_mul\_exp\_neg\_of\_nonpos} proves
$e^{-z}-(1-z+z^2/2)\le(-z)^3/2\cdot e^{-z}$, and the exported headline
\texttt{abs\_exp\_neg\_sub\_one\_add\_sub\_half\_sq\_le} proves $\le|z|^3/2\cdot\max(1,e^{-z})$ --- but
the section intro advertises, and the $z\ge0$ branch correctly proves, the sharp $1/6$. And $1/6$ is
genuinely achievable on $z\le0$: with $w=-z\ge0$, the Lagrange remainder gives
$e^{w}-(1+w+w^2/2)=e^{\xi}w^3/6\le e^{w}w^3/6=(|z|^3/6)e^{-z}$. So the Lean settles for a bound three
times looser than both the mathematics and its own documentation. (A companion docstring also
cross-references a non-existent declaration name in the same section.)

This is a \emph{fidelity} finding, not a math error: the $1/2$ bound is true. But it does not match the
claimed/intended $1/6$. The review-tide rule is unambiguous here --- \textbf{do not silently rewrite
the mathematics, and do not quietly correct the docstring down to $1/2$} (which would erase the very
signal that the Lean under-delivers). It is recorded, surfaced to the user, and left for them to
resolve: either strengthen the $z\le0$ branch and the headline to the sharp $1/6$ (a re-proof --- a
forward tide), or consciously accept $1/2$ and correct the section intro/docstrings accordingly. After
$\sim 50$ clean laplace reviews this is the first such escalation.

\section{Simplifications applied}
\textbf{One dead open --- and a triage the build corrected both ways.} \texttt{open Real} is dead
(every \texttt{Real} name is qualified, including the lemma names \texttt{Real.add\_one\_le\_exp},
\texttt{Real.hasDerivAt\_exp}, \texttt{Real.exp\_pos}); removed, build green. Instructively, my
pre-build triage had \emph{both} opens wrong: I guessed \texttt{open Set} was dead (it is live --- bare
\texttt{Ici} in the half-line case splits, which my interval grep had simply not listed) and hedged
that \texttt{open Real} might be live (it is dead). Removing \texttt{open Set} broke the build with
\texttt{Unknown identifier Ici}; removing \texttt{open Real} did not. The build is the arbiter, for the
reviewer's grep and the author's intuition alike.

\section{Disagreements and open questions}
The second-order constant (above) is the open question for the user. GPT also listed several
\texttt{*\_nonneg} locals as ``syntactically unreferenced'' but hedged that automation may consume
them implicitly; on this \texttt{positivity}/\texttt{nlinarith}-heavy file that risk is real (the same
implicit-consumption trap as the \reviewref[QuarticBoundedPrior review]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-oned-quarticboundedprior}'s
\texttt{ha}/\texttt{htail\_nn}), so they were left report-only for a build-arbitrated hygiene pass
rather than removed speculatively.

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{A true-but-loose constant is a fidelity finding, not a free docstring fix.} When a
    theorem proves $1/2$ and its docstring claims $1/6$, the green build proves only that $1/2$ holds
    --- it says nothing about which number was \emph{intended}. Correcting the docstring down to $1/2$
    would silently ratify the weaker theorem; strengthening the theorem to $1/6$ is new mathematics.
    Either way it is the user's call, and the reviewer's job is to surface the gap, not close it.
  \item \textbf{An interval grep is only as good as its list of interval names.} \texttt{Ici} was live
    and unlisted; the ``\texttt{open Set} is dead'' guess was wrong because the grep never looked for
    it. The build caught it instantly --- which is exactly why the open-removal verdict is always
    build-arbitrated, never grep-final.
\end{itemize}

\section{Follow-ups}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{User decision required:} the second-order $1/6$-vs-$1/2$ constant. If $1/6$ is wanted,
    a forward tide re-proves the $z\le0$ branch (Lagrange remainder $e^{\xi}w^3/6$) and propagates to
    the headline; the cross-reference and section intro then become consistent.
  \item Batched-hygiene tide: build-arbitrate the \texttt{*\_nonneg} dead-local candidates; narrow
    \texttt{import Mathlib}; extract the duplicated $z\le0$ nonnegativity proof.
\end{itemize}
