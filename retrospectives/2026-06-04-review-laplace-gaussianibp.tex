\begin{abstract}
A review of \texttt{Laplace/Multi/GaussianIBP.lean}, the multivariate Gaussian second-moment identity
$\int u_i u_j\,e^{-\frac12 u^\top H u} = Z\,\Sigma_{ij}$ (covariance $=H^{-1}$). The formula-level
mathematics is \textbf{clean} --- GPT-5.5 Pro found no sign, index, or coefficient error in the IBP, the
second-moment/inverse-entry identity, the bilinear form, or the odd-moment vanishing, and the
right-inverse-plus-injective $\Rightarrow$ two-sided-inverse step is sound. But the review surfaces a
\textbf{report-only escalation}: the docstring says the file \emph{proves} the identity ``by
coordinatewise integration by parts'', whereas the load-bearing IBP step is an \emph{undischarged assumed
hypothesis} (\texttt{FubiniIBPHypothesis}, which is literally the integrated IBP identity), never derived
from the symmetric/coercive structure or the present-but-unconnected slice machinery --- consistent with
the file being in-progress Phase-4/5 scaffolding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
A breadth pivot into \texttt{laplace} (the original SRI Lean seabed, the Susceptibility Primer's covariance
program), only two files previously reviewed. \texttt{Multi/GaussianIBP} is the analytic core --- the
multivariate Gaussian moment identity underlying \texttt{lem:laplace\_cov} --- chosen as a high
fidelity-stakes target (IBP is exactly where sign/coefficient errors hide), and clear of the in-flight
\texttt{anharmonic-*}/\texttt{partition-deriv} tides. It vets as $0$ \texttt{sorry} and not
skeleton-keyworded; the catch (below) is that $0$ \texttt{sorry} did not mean complete.

\section{What was reviewed}
The headline theorems: \texttt{gaussian\_ibp\_coord} ($\int u_j(Hu)_i\,gW=\delta_{ij}Z$),
\texttt{gaussian\_ibp\_column}, \texttt{gaussian\_second\_moment\_eq\_inverse\_entry\{,\_scalar\}}
($\int u_k u_j\,gW = Z\,\Sigma_{kj}$, $\Sigma=H^{-1}$), \texttt{gaussian\_dot\_mul\_dot} (bilinear), and
the odd-moment vanishing lemmas. Checked against the standard Gaussian moments
($\mathbb E[u_iu_j]=(H^{-1})_{ij}$, IBP from $\partial_{u_i}e^{-Q/2}=-(Hu)_ie^{-Q/2}$) and the Primer's
covariance-$=$-inverse-Hessian anchor.

\section{Fidelity findings}
\textbf{Formula-level: clean.} Every moment identity is mathematically correct, with the right Kronecker
delta, sign, and column/entry orientation; the two-sided-inverse derivation (from $H\circ\Sigma=\mathrm{id}$
plus injectivity of $H$) is sound; the odd-moment vanishing is the usual $u\mapsto-u$ argument. The
theorem \emph{signatures} are honest --- each takes the assumed hypotheses explicitly.

\textbf{Escalation (report-only): the docstring overstates the proof architecture.} The module docstring
states the file \emph{proves} the second-moment identity ``by coordinatewise integration by parts'', but:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{FubiniIBPHypothesis}~$H\,i\,j$ is \emph{defined} as the integrated IBP identity itself,
    $\int(\delta_{ij}\,gW - u_j(Hu)_i\,gW)=0$, and is \emph{only ever assumed} (as \texttt{h\_fubini} in
    every moment theorem) and consumed by \texttt{unfold}. I verified there is \emph{no} theorem with
    conclusion \texttt{: FubiniIBPHypothesis} --- it is never discharged.
  \item So \texttt{gaussian\_ibp\_coord} is an \emph{algebraic rearrangement} of the assumed identity,
    not a derivation from symmetry/coercivity/FTC/Fubini. The slice machinery
    (\texttt{integral\_sliceDeriv\_eq\_zero}) that would discharge it is present but \emph{not connected}.
  \item The symmetric/coercive hypotheses named in the docstring do not appear in the main moment-theorem
    statements (only in local lemmas). The main theorems are therefore \emph{conditional}: given the
    assumed IBP identity and integrability, the moments follow.
\end{itemize}
\emph{In fairness:} the signatures make the assumption explicit (not a hidden gap), and the commit history
(``Phase 4'', ``Phase 5.4k'') and the sibling \texttt{CovarianceExplicit} ``(skeleton, in progress)''
indicate an active multi-phase construction --- \texttt{FubiniIBPHypothesis} is plausibly the known current
frontier. So the docstring describes the \emph{target} while the IBP step is, for now, a hypothesis. The
user adjudicates: qualify the docstring (``modulo the assumed Fubini-IBP identity''), or complete the
slice$\to$Fubini discharge. Minor sub-notes: the theorems give \emph{unnormalised} raw moments
($\int u_iu_j\,gW=Z\Sigma_{ij}$), not the normalised covariance $\tfrac1Z\int=\Sigma$; and the docstring's
``$\Sigma$ inverse witness ($H\circ\Sigma=\mathrm{id}$)'' is only a right inverse (the theorem correctly
adds injectivity).

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{sorry}-free $\ne$ complete.} This file has $0$ \texttt{sorry} yet leaves its hardest
    step --- the multivariate Fubini/IBP identity --- as an \emph{assumed \texttt{Prop} hypothesis}
    (\texttt{FubiniIBPHypothesis}) that is essentially the conclusion. Review-vetting that screens only on
    \texttt{sorry}/skeleton keywords will admit such files; the sharper screen is ``is any load-bearing
    hypothesis an assumed \texttt{Prop} that encodes the conclusion?''. This is the laplace-Multi analogue
    of the qd \texttt{Definition1} framework gaps.
  \item \textbf{Honest signatures, overstated prose.} The contrast with a genuine defect: here the formal
    statements are faithful (the assumption is explicit), and only the docstring narrative oversells. The
    review's value is naming precisely where ``proves by IBP'' becomes ``assumes the IBP identity''.
\end{itemize}
