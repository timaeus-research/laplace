\begin{abstract}
Two nonnegative losses whose Laplace families agree projectively beyond
all orders, $\int\phi\,e^{-tL_2} - C(t)\int\phi\,e^{-tL_1} =
o(t^{-\infty})$ for every $\phi \in C_c^\infty$ and an arbitrary scalar
function $C$, have the same zero set as soon as both have a zero; the
scalar is polynomially tame and eventually positive; and once the losses
agree near one zero, which the morning's germ theorem supplies at any
common analytic zero, $C(t) = 1 + o(t^{-\infty})$ and the two families
agree \emph{exactly} beyond all orders at every tested observable. With
$C = Z_2/Z_1$ this closes the germbij note's normalized story: equality
of the normalized expansions $\Phi_{L_1} = \Phi_{L_2}$ for smooth
nonnegative losses analytic at their zeros gives equal zero sets, local
equality on an open neighbourhood of the zero set, $Z_2/Z_1 = 1 +
o(t^{-\infty})$, and exact agreement of every moment. The load-bearing
new lemma is the anchor, $\int\psi\,e^{-tK} \ge \kappa\,t^{-d/2}$ near a
$C^2$ zero, the $a \equiv 1$ case of the seabed's sector bound; it
discharges a polynomial lower bound that three seabed theorems had
carried as a hypothesis since the anchoring tide. Six hundred and
fifty lines, two LSP rounds, no analyticity anywhere except through the
germ theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

The morning's tide,
\tideref[2026-09-19-06-05]{2026-09-19-06-05-tide-germbij-normalized},
proved that projective agreement forces $L_1 = L_2$ near every common
analytic zero, whatever $C$ is, and recorded two stretch corollaries from
its consult: that equal zero loci are forced without analyticity, and
that the scalar must be $1 + o(t^{-\infty})$. Both rest on one missing
estimate. The seabed's scalar-gauge algebra in \code{Anchoring} and
\code{OnePointAnchoring} (\code{superPoly\_of\_mul\_anchor},
\code{superPoly\_sub\_of\_scalar\_gauge},
\code{anchored\_proportionality\_remove\_scalar},
\code{one\_point\_anchoring\_contradiction}) is complete modulo a
hypothesis \code{hanchor\_low}: a positive polynomial lower bound on the
reference moment $\int\psi\,e^{-tL_1}$ at an anchoring bump. Nothing in
the seabed proved such a bound, although the sector lower bound
\code{sector\_lower\_bound\_multi} contains it as the degenerate case
$a \equiv 1$, $m = 0$. The deliberation (tide log
\code{tide-log/2026-09-19-15-40-tide-germbij-projective-closure.md},
consult in \code{gpt\_germbij\_projective\_v1.md}) settled on packaging
that anchor, the complementary exponential gap bound away from the zero
set, and the four consequences: tameness of $C$, equal zero loci, scalar
rigidity with the transfer to exact agreement, and the $1/Z$ headline.
Both parties voted for the whole package.

\section{The thing formalised}

Write $I_i(\phi, t) = \int\phi\,e^{-tL_i}$ and call the families
\emph{projectively agreeing} (\code{ProjectiveAgreement L1 L2 C}) when
$I_2(\phi) - C\,I_1(\phi) = o(t^{-\infty})$ for every smooth compactly
supported $\phi$.

\begin{quote}\itshape
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(G)] If $K \ge 0$ is continuous, $K(p) = 0$, $K$ is $C^2$ at $p$,
and $\psi \ge 0$ is continuous, compactly supported, and equal to $1$
near $p$, then $\int\psi\,e^{-tK} \ge \kappa\,t^{-d/2}$ for large $t$.
\item[(E)] If $L \ge \delta > 0$ on the support of $\psi$, then
$|\int\psi\,e^{-tL}| \le \|\psi\|_1 e^{-t\delta}$ for $t \ge 0$, and
$B(t)\int\psi\,e^{-tL} = o(t^{-\infty})$ for any polynomially bounded
$B$.
\item[(T)] For continuous nonnegative $L_1, L_2$ agreeing projectively:
a $C^2$ zero of $L_1$ gives $|C(t)| \le A\,t^{d}$ eventually; a $C^2$
zero of $L_2$ gives $C(t) \ge c\,t^{-d} > 0$ eventually.
\item[(Z)] If moreover both losses are $C^2$ near their zeros and both
have a zero, their zero sets coincide.
\item[(R)] If $L_1 = L_2$ near some $C^2$ zero $p$ of $L_1$, then
$C(t) = 1 + o(t^{-\infty})$, and $I_2(\phi) - I_1(\phi) =
o(t^{-\infty})$ for every tested $\phi$.
\item[(N)] For smooth nonnegative losses analytic at their zeros, both
with zeros, and a nonnegative window $\chi$ with positive partition
values: if the normalized families agree beyond all orders for every
tested $\phi$, then the zero sets coincide, $L_1 = L_2$ on an open
neighbourhood of the zero set, $Z_2/Z_1 = 1 + o(t^{-\infty})$, and
$I_2(\phi) - I_1(\phi) = o(t^{-\infty})$ for every tested $\phi$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{quote}

\section{Proof strategy}

\emph{The anchor.} Translate $p$ to the origin, take $S$ the closed unit
ball, $a \equiv 1$, $m = 0$, $c = 1$ in \code{sector\_lower\_bound\_multi}:
on the scaled ball $t^{-1/2}S$ the bump is $1$ once $t \ge 4/R^2$, the
quadratic upper bound $K \le C_0\|w\|^2$ comes from
\code{quadratic\_upper\_bound\_of\_nonneg}, and
\code{setIntegral\_le\_integral} compares with the full nonnegative
integral. The constant is $\mathrm{vol}(S)\,e^{-4C_0}$ and the exponent
$-d/2$. An integer-power wrapper $\kappa\,t^{-d}$ for $t \ge 1$ matches
the shape of \code{hanchor\_low}.

\emph{The gap.} $|\int\psi\,e^{-tL}| \le \int|\psi|\,e^{-tL} \le
\|\psi\|_1 e^{-t\delta}$, pointwise on the support. For the product
with a polynomially bounded $B$, Mathlib's
\code{isLittleO\_pow\_exp\_pos\_mul\_atTop} gives $t^n \le
e^{(\delta/2)t}$ eventually, so
\[|B\cdot I| \le A\|\psi\|_1 e^{-(\delta/2)t},\]
and the seabed's
\code{superPoly\_of\_eventually\_abs\_le\_exp} finishes.

\emph{Tameness.} At a zero $p$ of $L_1$ with bump $\psi_p$,
\[|C|\,I_1(\psi_p) \le |I_2(\psi_p)| + |R_{\psi_p}| \le M + 1\]
eventually, and the anchor gives $I_1(\psi_p) \ge \kappa\,t^{-d}$. At a zero $q$ of
$L_2$: $C\,I_1(\psi_q) = I_2(\psi_q) - R_{\psi_q} \ge \tfrac{\kappa}{2}
t^{-d}$ eventually, and $I_1(\psi_q) \le M$, so $C \ge
\tfrac{\kappa}{2M}t^{-d} > 0$.

\emph{Equal zero loci.} If $L_1(p) = 0 < L_2(p)$, a bump in the gap of
$L_2$ makes $I_2$ exponentially small, so $C\,I_1(\psi_p)$ is beyond all
orders and the anchor makes $C$ itself beyond all orders
(\code{superPoly\_of\_mul\_anchor} applied to $C + 1$); then every
$I_2(\phi) = R_\phi + C\,I_1(\phi)$ is beyond all orders, contradicting
the anchor at any zero of $L_2$. If $L_2(q) = 0 < L_1(q)$, the upper
tameness bound makes $C\,I_1(\psi_q)$ beyond all orders by (E), so
$I_2(\psi_q)$ is, contradicting the anchor at $q$.

\emph{Rigidity and transfer.} A bump $\psi_0$ inside the agreement
neighbourhood has $I_1(\psi_0) = I_2(\psi_0)$ exactly
(\code{anchor\_moment\_eq}), so $R_{\psi_0} = -(C - 1)\,I_1(\psi_0)$;
the anchor and \code{superPoly\_of\_mul\_anchor} give $C - 1 =
o(t^{-\infty})$, and \code{superPoly\_sub\_of\_scalar\_gauge} against
the bounded $I_1(\phi)$ removes the gauge from every tested moment.

\emph{The headline.} Normalized agreement gives projective agreement
with $C = Z_2/Z_1$ because $Z_2$ is bounded for $t \ge 0$ (the block of
the morning's $1/Z$ theorem, now a standalone lemma
\code{projectiveAgreement\_of\_normalized}); then (Z), the locus form of
the germ theorem at $W_0 = \{L_1 = 0\}$, and (R) at any zero.

\section{Roadblocks and resolutions}

None of substance. The two LSP rounds fixed: an implicit centre $p$ the
elaborator could not infer through the quadratic-bound lemma (passed as
\code{(p := p)}); a \code{simp} that had already reduced $1^2\cdot$ in
the sector bound's integrand, so the \code{calc} step had to name the
reduced form; a beta-redex in front of a rewrite inside
\code{SuperPoly.congr} (the standing \code{beta\_reduce} gotcha); and
\code{ContDiff.of\_le (by simp)} for $2 \le \infty$, which does not fire
on this pin. The last was resolved by design rather than by finding the
coercion lemma: the closure theorems take $C^2$ at the zeros as a
hypothesis, and the analytic corollaries discharge it with
\code{AnalyticAt.contDiffAt}, which is what the germ theorem already
assumes.

\emph{The consult}, before writing, confirmed every statement and
sharpened three things. On the scalar:
\gptbox{There is no issue with negative or nonmeasurable $C$. All
estimates involving $C(t)$ are pointwise in $t$; you never integrate
$C$. In particular $|C(t)|\,I_1(\psi,t) \le |I_2(\psi,t)| +
|R_\psi(t)|$ gives the upper polynomial bound without a sign
assumption.}
On what the transfer does and does not give:
\gptbox{Thus the immediate conclusion is for $C_c^\infty$, not
automatically $C_c^0$. However, with globally smooth losses, the
continuous-test conclusion is indeed true. [\ldots] This proves local
total-variation agreement beyond all orders, hence agreement against
every continuous compactly supported observable. I would formalize that
as a separate theorem.}
And on the normalized/projective bridge:
\gptbox{For a fixed compactly supported window, nonnegative losses make
$Z_2$ bounded, so normalized SuperPoly agreement implies projective
agreement. The converse requires control of $1/Z_2$. A positive window
supported away from all zeros can have exponentially small $Z_2$.}
The file follows all three: (T) has no sign hypothesis, the conclusions
are stated for the tested class only, and (N) uses the one direction
that holds.

\section{Lessons}

\begin{itemize}
\item For \code{CLAUDE.md}: \code{ContDiff.of\_le} from $\infty$ to a
finite grade has no \code{by simp} discharge on this pin. Where an
analyticity hypothesis is in scope, \code{AnalyticAt.contDiffAt} is the
route; otherwise take the finite grade as a hypothesis and let callers
discharge it.
\item For the tide skill: a hypothesis that several seabed theorems
share and none discharges (\code{hanchor\_low}, three consumers, one
month) is a tide in itself. The survey step should grep for repeated
undischarged hypotheses, not only for open questions in the paper.
\item For the paper: the headline is ``at a common analytic zero,
projective agreement fixes its own gauge''. State the scope: modulo
$o(t^{-\infty})$, for the tested class, and only in the presence of a
zero on each side ($L_2 = L_1 + c$ is projectively identical to $L_1$
with $C = e^{-ct}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Follow-ups}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Local total variation.} $\int_K|e^{-tL_2} - e^{-tL_1}| =
o(t^{-\infty})$ for compact $K$, via $|a - b|^2 \le t\,D\,(a - b)$ with
$D = L_2 - L_1$ tested at the smooth observable $\eta^2 D$. Upgrades the
transfer to continuous and bounded measurable observables. Needs smooth
losses, not analyticity.
\item \emph{The converse bridge.} Projective agreement implies
normalized agreement when the window is positive near a zero, by the
anchor applied to $Z_2$.
\item \emph{Retire the anchor hypothesis.} Restate the one-point
anchoring contradiction and the packaged gauge removal of
\code{Anchoring} with the lower bound discharged internally.
\item \emph{Two-sided tameness as one statement,} $c\,t^{-d/2} \le C(t)
\le A\,t^{d/2}$ with the sharp half-integer exponents; the file has the
integer-exponent forms.
\item \emph{Paper.} Promote (Z), (R), (N) to the note as the closing
theorems of the normalized section; Proposition~7.8 (one-point
anchoring) becomes a corollary of (R).
\end{itemize}
