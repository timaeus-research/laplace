\begin{abstract}
Towards a uniform Taylor tree. Under a Gaussian envelope $|c(s)|\le K(1+s)^De^{\beta sa'}$ the log
moments obey $|M[\alpha;\ell;c]|\le K\int_0^\infty s^{\alpha-1}(1+|\log s|)^\ell e^{-\beta s^2}(1+s)^De^{\beta sa'}ds$
(\texttt{abs\_logMoment\_le\_of\_env}); a discarded pole term obeys
$|N^{-\alpha}(A\log N+B)|\le(|A|+|B|)N^{-2T}(1+\log N)$ for $\alpha\ge2T$, $N\ge1$
(\texttt{discard\_explicit}); and for the analytic amplitude with envelope $(C_0,L,D,\rho)$ the
canonical coefficient functions $U_\alpha,V_\alpha,C_\alpha$ have explicit envelopes
(\texttt{canonUEnv}, \texttt{canonVEnv}, \texttt{canonCEnv}), whence
$|A_\alpha|\le\mathrm{canonCEnv}(\alpha)\,EM(\alpha,0)$ and
$|B_\alpha|\le\mathrm{canonUEnv}(\alpha)EM(\alpha,0)+\mathrm{canonVEnv}(\alpha)EM(\alpha,0)+\mathrm{canonCEnv}(\alpha)EM(\alpha,1)$
(\texttt{canonA\_abs\_le\_env}, \texttt{canonB\_abs\_le\_env}), all constants depending only on
the envelope data. Zero \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{axiom}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Mathematics}

$|\int f|\le\int|f|$ and $|\log s|^\ell\le(1+|\log s|)^\ell$ give the tail-moment bound; the
pointwise envelope then gives the Gaussian-moment bound (unit~132). The canonical $u$-face
coefficient at a $u$-pole is $k_1^{-1}\mathrm{FP}_\gamma$ at the canonical truncation order, so its
envelope is the finite-part envelope of unit~132 with the analytic adapter's Taylor-data and
remainder envelopes at that order; the collision coefficient $c_{ij}/(k_1k_2)$ has envelope
$C_0\rho^{-i-j}/(k_1k_2)$. Off the poles the coefficients vanish and the envelope constants are set
to zero.

\section{Paper-facing meaning}

This provides the missing uniform bound on the discarded pole terms of the Taylor tree (Astra~\#9:
``\texttt{discard\_isBigO} needs uniform coefficient bounds''), so that the ``for every $T$''
statement can be made uniform over the analytic family with a constant $K_T$ depending only on the
envelope data and $T$. It also bounds the canonical coefficients themselves in terms of the envelope,
the first half of Astra's R6 (coefficient maps bounded on the weighted family).
