\begin{abstract}
Let $P$ be a real symmetric positive definite precision matrix with
eigenvalues $p_i$ and let $h > 0$ satisfy $h\,p_i < 2$ for every $i$. The
Euler-discretised Langevin chain $w \leftarrow (I - hP)\,w + \sqrt{2h}\,\xi$
has a unique stationary covariance, namely
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{ULA}} = (P - \tfrac{h}{2}P^2)^{-1}$; it is positive
definite, commutes with $P$, and has eigenvalues
$1/(p_i(1 - hp_i/2))$. If the noise covariance is instead
$2hI + h^2t^2C$ for an arbitrary matrix $C$, the unique stationary
covariance has entries
$(2h\delta_{ij} + h^2t^2\tilde C_{ij})/(h(p_i + p_j) - h^2p_ip_j)$ in the
eigenbasis of $P$, whose diagonal is the ULA variance times
$1 + ht^2\tilde C_{ii}/2$. The iterates of the covariance recursion from
any start satisfy $\Sigma_k - \Sigma_\infty = A^k(\Sigma_0 -
\Sigma_\infty)(A^\top)^k$, which gives $\Sigma_\infty(I - A^{2k})$ from a
zero start and $\sigma^2(1 - \rho^{2k})$ in one dimension. On the Gibbs
side, in the seabed's own language, the expectation of $K = \tfrac12
w^\top Hw$ under the weight $\exp(-tK - \gamma|w|^2/2)$ is $\tfrac12
\tr\big(H(tH + \gamma I)^{-1}\big)$, and $t\langle K\rangle = d/2$ when
$\gamma = 0$. These are the closed forms that the note \emph{Sanity on
Sampling} checks stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics against.
Three new modules, 715 lines, no \code{sorry}, \code{axiom} or
\code{native\_decide}; one deliberation round in which GPT-6 Astra
strengthened two of the three candidates; a single session.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

The note \emph{Sanity on Sampling} (paper \code{sanity} in the SRI
registry, Overleaf clone \code{learning-theory/papers/sanity}, code in
\code{timaeus-research/sanity-sampling}) compares two ways of computing
posterior covariances and the local learning coefficient for
nondegenerate potentials: the inverse Hessian, through the Laplace
formulas of the Susceptibility Primer, and sampling with SGLD. Its
experiments E1 to E8 rest on a small set of closed forms for the
Gaussian linear recursion: the ULA law (its equation \code{eq:ula}), the
Lyapunov law with minibatch noise (\code{eq:mblaw}), the AR(1) variance
of a finite chain, and the Gaussian identities for the localised LLC
(\code{eq:llc}). All of them were derived by hand in the note and
checked numerically; none had been formalised.

The seabed is the \code{laplace} repository at commit \code{b7e20dd}. It
carries the Gibbs objects (\code{partitionFunction},
\code{gibbsExpectation}, \code{gibbsCov}), the multivariate Gaussian
weight \code{gaussianWeight}, the quadratic form \code{quadForm}, and
the Gaussian integration by parts theorem
\code{gaussian\_second\_moment\_eq\_inverse\_entry}, which says that the
second moment of $e^{-\frac12 u^\top Pu}$ is $Z\,(P^{-1})_{ij}$. It has
nothing on discrete-time samplers, Markov chains or Lyapunov equations,
so this tide opens a new arc, \code{Laplace/Sampler/}.

The deliberation is recorded in \code{tide-log/}: the log entry carries
the same date stamp as this retrospective, and the consult is
\code{gpt\_sampler\_laws\_v1.md}. It started from three candidates: the Lyapunov laws as linear algebra, the Gaussian
invariance of the ULA step as a probabilistic statement, and the Gaussian
LLC identity in the seabed's language. GPT-6 Astra confirmed all three,
deferred the probabilistic one as measure-theoretic plumbing, and
strengthened the other two: the minibatch formula needs no commutation
between $C$ and $P$, and the finite-time identity holds for any starting
covariance without any commutation. It also fixed the route:
\gptbox{Route: spectral, not Neumann. Diagonal Lyapunov solver
$X_{ij} = N_{ij}/(1 - a_ia_j)$ for $|a_i| < 1$, transported through an
orthogonal diagonalisation; existence and uniqueness become scalar
algebra. State the stability hypothesis eigenvalue-wise: the sup norm on
$\iota \to \R$ does not give $\|I - hP\|_2 < 1$.}
Both parties voted for the six-item cluster after one round.

\section{The thing formalised}

Write $A = I - hP$ for the ULA step, $\mathcal T_N(X) = AXA^\top + N$ for
the covariance step, and $U$ for an orthogonal matrix with $P = U\,
\mathrm{diag}(p)\,U^\top$.

\begin{quote}\itshape
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(L)] For $a \in \R^d$ with $|a_i| < 1$ and any $N$, the equation
$X = \mathrm{diag}(a)\,X\,\mathrm{diag}(a) + N$ has the unique solution
$X_{ij} = N_{ij}/(1 - a_ia_j)$. For $A = U\,\mathrm{diag}(a)\,U^\top$ with
$U$ orthogonal, $X = AXA^\top + N$ has the unique solution
$U\,\big[(U^\top NU)_{ij}/(1 - a_ia_j)\big]\,U^\top$.
\item[(U)] For $P$ symmetric positive definite and $0 < h$ with
$hp_i < 2$: $\Sigma_{\mathrm{ULA}} = (P - \tfrac h2 P^2)^{-1}$ is the
unique solution of $\Sigma = A\Sigma A + 2hI$, it is positive definite,
and $(U^\top\Sigma_{\mathrm{ULA}}U)_{ij} = \delta_{ij}/(p_i(1 - hp_i/2))$.
\item[(M)] With $N = 2hI + h^2t^2C$ and $\tilde C = U^\top CU$, the
unique solution has $(U^\top\Sigma U)_{ij} = (2h\delta_{ij} +
h^2t^2\tilde C_{ij})/(h(p_i + p_j) - h^2p_ip_j)$, and on the diagonal
$(1 + ht^2\tilde C_{ii}/2)/(p_i(1 - hp_i/2))$.
\item[(F)] If $S$ is a fixed point of $\mathcal T_N$ then
$\mathcal T_N^{\,k}(X_0) - S = A^k(X_0 - S)(A^\top)^k$; for $X_0 = 0$,
$A$ symmetric and $AS = SA$ this is $S(I - A^{2k})$; in one dimension the
variance after $k$ steps is $\frac{v}{1 - \rho^2}(1 - \rho^{2k})$.
\item[(G)] For $P = tH + \gamma I$ with the seabed's integrability
package,
\[
\frac{1}{Z(P)}\int \tfrac12\langle u, Hu\rangle\,
e^{-\frac12\langle u, Pu\rangle}\,du = \tfrac12\sum_{ij}H_{ij}(P^{-1})_{ij},
\]
and for symmetric $H$ and $\gamma = 0$ the right side is $d/(2t)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{quote}

($U$ is \code{orthoOf hP}, the real orthogonal matrix packaged from
Mathlib's \code{eigenvectorUnitary}):

and its second moments, and the Fubini hypothesis of the integration by
parts theorem, all stated abstractly rather than derived from positive
definiteness of $P$.

\section{Proof strategy}

\emph{Diagonal solver and transport.} For $D = \mathrm{diag}(a)$ the
covariance step acts entrywise, $(DXD + N)_{ij} = a_ia_jX_{ij} + N_{ij}$,
so a fixed point is a scalar equation per entry with the unique solution
$N_{ij}/(1 - a_ia_j)$ because $1 - a_ia_j > 0$ when $|a_i|, |a_j| < 1$.
Conjugation by an orthogonal $U$ intertwines the steps,
$U^\top\mathcal T_{A,N}(X)U = \mathcal T_{D,\,U^\top NU}(U^\top XU)$
(\code{covStep\_conj}), and is a bijection on matrices, so the fixed
points of the general step are the transports of the diagonal ones. This
is \code{lyapunovVia\_fixed\_iff}. Mathlib's spectral theorem for
Hermitian matrices produces a unitary over $\R$; \code{orthoOf} coerces
it to a real matrix and \code{spectral\_real} restates
$A = U\,\mathrm{diag}(\lambda)\,U^\top$ using
\code{conjTranspose\_eq\_transpose\_of\_trivial} to turn the star into a
transpose.

\emph{ULA closed form.} The step satisfies $I - A^2 = 2hP\,(I -
\tfrac h2P) = 2h\,(P - \tfrac h2P^2)$, a two-line \code{module} identity
(\code{one\_sub\_ulaStep\_sq}). Since $\Sigma_{\mathrm{ULA}}$ is the
inverse of a polynomial in $P$ it commutes with $P$
(\code{ulaCov\_commute}, from \code{mul\_nonsing\_inv} and
\code{nonsing\_inv\_mul}), hence with $A$, so $\Sigma - A\Sigma A = (I -
A^2)\Sigma = 2h\,(P - \tfrac h2P^2)\Sigma = 2hI$. This is
\code{ulaCov\_fixed}; it needs only $P^\top = P$ and the invertibility of
$P - \tfrac h2P^2$. Under the stability hypothesis the determinant is
$\prod_i p_i \prod_i(1 - hp_i/2) \ne 0$ (\code{isUnit\_det\_ulaDenom},
through \code{det\_eq\_prod\_eigenvalues} and \code{ulaStep\_eq\_conj}),
so uniqueness follows from \code{lyapunovVia\_fixed\_iff}, and the
eigenbasis entries are $2h/(1 - (1 - hp_i)^2) = 1/(p_i(1 - hp_i/2))$.
Positivity is \code{PosDef.diagonal} for the diagonal solution and
\code{PosDef.conjTranspose\_mul\_mul\_same} for its transport.

\emph{Minibatch entries.} With $N = 2hI + h^2t^2C$ the transported
formula gives $(2h\delta_{ij} + h^2t^2\tilde C_{ij})/(1 - (1 - hp_i)(1 -
hp_j))$, and the denominator is $h(p_i + p_j) - h^2p_ip_j$ by \code{ring}.
No commutation is used anywhere; the diagonal specialisation is a
\code{div\_eq\_div\_iff} plus \code{ring}.

\emph{Finite time.} $\mathcal T_N(Y) - S = A(Y - S)A^\top$ for a fixed
point $S$, so induction with \code{Function.iterate\_succ\_apply'} gives
$A^k(X_0 - S)(A^\top)^k$. With $X_0 = 0$, $A = A^\top$ and $AS = SA$ the
right side is $-S A^{2k}$, whence $S(I - A^{2k})$; the scalar AR(1) case
is the same induction on $\R$.

\emph{Gaussian LLC.} Writing $u = \sum_j u_j e_j$ (the seabed's
\code{eq\_sum\_stdBasis}) gives $\langle u, Hu\rangle = \sum_{ij}H_{ij}
u_iu_j$; the integral of each term against the Gaussian weight is
$Z\,(P^{-1})_{ij}$ by \code{gaussian\_second\_moment\_eq\_inverse\_entry},
and the finite sums pass through the integral by
\code{integral\_finsetSum}. Dividing by $Z$ gives $\tfrac12\sum_{ij}
H_{ij}(P^{-1})_{ij}$. For $P = tH$ and $P^{-1} = \tfrac1t H^{-1}$ the
pairing collapses: $\sum_{ij}H_{ij}(H^{-1})_{ij} = \sum_{ij}H_{ji}
(H^{-1})_{ij} = \tr(HH^{-1}) = d$ using the symmetry of $H$, so
$t\langle K\rangle = d/2$.

\section{Roadblocks and resolutions}

None of the mathematics resisted; the friction was all Lean idiom, and
most of it in the spectral packaging.
\begin{itemize}\raggedright
\item Mathlib's \code{Matrix.IsHermitian.spectral\_theorem} now reads
\[
A = \code{Unitary.conjStarAlgAut}\ \R\ \_\ U\
\big(\mathrm{diag}(\code{RCLike.ofReal} \circ \lambda)\big).
\]
Unfolding needs \code{Unitary.conjStarAlgAut\_apply}
(the \code{Matrix.}-prefixed name no longer exists), and the
$\code{RCLike.ofReal} \circ \lambda$ over $\R$ is closed by \code{congr 1}
alone; adding a \code{funext} or \code{simp} after it errors with ``no
goals''. The same ``no goals'' appeared twice more when \code{congr 1}
finished a goal that I had followed with \code{ring} bullets.
\item The diagonal solver's uniqueness reduced to $x = n/(1 - a^2)$ from
$x = a^2x + n$; \code{field\_simp} followed by \code{linear\_combination}
left a residue, and \code{rw [eq\_div\_iff hpos.ne']} followed by
\code{linear\_combination -h} closed it.
\item In the commuting finite-time identity, rewriting $S\,A^{2k}$ into
$S\,A^k\,A^k$ failed on associativity until the chain was ordered
\code{mul\_sub, mul\_one, hc, mul\_assoc, ← pow\_add, two\_mul}.
\item \code{simp only [quadForm, clm\_apply\_eq\_sum, Finset.mul\_sum]}
loops: the expansion of $(Hu)_i$ produces terms $(He_j)_i$ that match the
same lemma again. The fix is a targeted \code{rw} inside
\code{Finset.sum\_congr}.
\item Scalar cancellation orientation: the goal was $(\tfrac1t\cdot t)
\bullet (H \circ H^{-1}) = \mathrm{id}$, so \code{one\_div\_mul\_cancel},
not \code{mul\_one\_div\_cancel}.
\item After \code{split\_ifs} in the diagonal positivity computation the
$i = j$ branch still mentioned $\lambda_j$; \code{subst} then
\code{rw [mul\_one]} closed it.
\item Deprecations since the seabed's pin: \code{integral\_finset\_sum}
is now \code{integral\_finsetSum}, \code{integrable\_finset\_sum} is
\code{integrable\_finsetSum}, and \code{ContinuousLinearMap.smul\_apply}
resolves under the open namespace as \code{smul\_apply}.
\end{itemize}
The one architectural point worth recording: the deliberation proposed
stating stability as $\|A\|_2 < 1$ and noted that Mathlib's operator norm
on $\iota \to \R$ is the sup norm, which does not give it. Stating the
hypothesis eigenvalue-wise ($|1 - hp_i| < 1$, equivalently $0 < hp_i <
2$) avoided \code{EuclideanSpace} entirely, and every theorem is stated
on \code{Matrix} $\iota\,\iota\,\R$ with \code{Fintype} and \code{DecidableEq} instances on $\iota$.

\section{Lessons}

\begin{itemize}
\item For \code{CLAUDE.md}: the real spectral theorem recipe
(\code{orthoOf}, \code{spectral\_real}) and the \code{congr 1} closure
of $\code{RCLike.ofReal} \circ \lambda$ are worth a section; they will
recur in any matrix-valued tide. Landed in this commit.
\item For the lean-formalisation skill: for linear-algebraic targets, a
skeleton of typechecked \code{sorry} statements written in one pass and
then filled module by module worked with no rework; the only statement
change during filling was dropping an unused positivity hypothesis from
the determinant lemma.
\item For the tide skill: the direction was a six-item programme. Taking
the sampler-side cluster and one Gibbs-side bridge, and writing the
remaining items into the log as deferred, kept the tide to one coherent
excursion. The numerical check was free: the note's experiments E1 and
E8 had already tested every closed form at machine precision on the
exact-gradient cells.
\item For the paper: the Lean statements make explicit what the note
states loosely, that the minibatch law needs no commutation of $C_g$
with $P$ and that its diagonal inflation factor is exact for any $C_g$
in the eigenbasis of the precision.
\end{itemize}

\section{Follow-ups}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Gaussian invariance.} $N(0, \Sigma)$ pushed through one ULA
step is $N(0, A\Sigma A^\top + 2hI)$, via \code{isGaussian\_map} and
\code{isGaussian\_conv} on \code{EuclideanSpace} $\R\,\iota$; then
$N(0, \Sigma_{\mathrm{ULA}})$ is invariant.
\item \emph{Expected sample variance.} Temporal cross-covariances
$\Cov(x_{r+l}, x_r) = A^l\Sigma_r$ and the note's finite-chain formula
for the variance of $n$ consecutive draws (\code{ar1\_expected\_sample\_variance}
in the code).
\item \emph{Full state-dependent law.} $\Sigma = A\Sigma A + 2hI +
h^2t^2(C + c\sum_i(H_i - H)\Sigma(H_i - H))$, needing a contraction
hypothesis on the whole operator, not just on $A$.
\item \emph{Monotonicity.} $N_1 \preceq N_2$ implies $\Sigma(N_1) \preceq
\Sigma(N_2)$, hence $\Sigma_{\mathrm{mb}} \succeq \Sigma_{\mathrm{ULA}}$;
cheap from the entry formula.
\item \emph{Operator-norm wrapper.} $\|I - hP\|_2 < 1$ on
\code{EuclideanSpace} equivalent to the eigenvalue-wise hypothesis.
\item \emph{Discharging the Gibbs hypotheses.} Derive the integrability
and Fubini package of (G) from $P$ positive definite, so that
\code{localised\_gaussian\_K\_expectation} takes \code{hP : P.PosDef}
alone.
\item \emph{One-loop coefficient and Rosenbrock.} The $t^{-2}$ covariance
correction of experiment E7 and the $d = 2$ Rosenbrock closed forms of
E5, both from the direction, both untouched.
\item \emph{Paper tags.} Proposed \code{\textbackslash leanref} markers
for \code{eq:ula}, \code{eq:mblaw} and \code{eq:llc} of the note are
staged in \code{learning-theory/projects/sanity/staging/}; landing them
in the Overleaf clone is a separate step.
\end{itemize}
