\begin{abstract}
A soundness review of \texttt{Laplace/Multi/QuadraticApprox.lean}: the setup for the multivariate Laplace
quadratic approximation --- the quadratic form $\mathrm{quadForm}\,H\,z=\langle z,Hz\rangle$, the rescaled
perturbation $s_t(u)=t\,L(u/\sqrt t)-\tfrac12\langle u,Hu\rangle$, and the definitional rescaling identity
$t\,L(u/\sqrt t)=\tfrac12\langle u,Hu\rangle+s_t(u)$. Result: \textbf{a clean fidelity pass with one landed
edit} --- a redundant Mathlib import, removed under rebuild-arbitration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
This is the foundational setup file for the primer's Stage~3 multivariate covariance theorem
(\texttt{lem:laplace\_cov}): it names the quadratic form, the rescaled remainder, and the algebraic identity
that splits $t\,L(u/\sqrt t)$ into its Gaussian part plus the remainder, all of which the downstream
\texttt{GaussianIBP} and \texttt{Covariance} files consume. It is the multivariate analogue of the
one-dimensional \texttt{Laplace.OneD.Rescaling} setup, and the second small \texttt{Multi/} file reviewed in
succession after \reviewref[AffineBilinearCov]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-multi-affine-bilinear-cov}.

\section{What was reviewed}
Two definitions (\texttt{quadForm}, \texttt{rescaledPerturbation}), the \texttt{rescaling\_identity} theorem,
and two \texttt{rfl} unfolding lemmas. The fidelity surface is minimal by design --- these are definitions and
one definitional identity --- which is exactly why it is worth confirming the definitions say what the
downstream proofs assume they say.

\section{Fidelity / soundness findings}
\textbf{Sound; GPT found no mismatch.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \texttt{quadForm H z = $\sum_i$ z i * (H z) i} is the standard quadratic form $\langle z,Hz\rangle$ on
    $\iota\to\mathbb R$.
  \item \texttt{rescaledPerturbation} has the correct sign and factor,
    $t\,L((\sqrt t)^{-1}\bullet u)-\tfrac12\,\mathrm{quadForm}\,H\,u$, and $(\sqrt t)^{-1}\bullet u$ is the
    right Lean encoding of the vector $u/\sqrt t$ (scalar multiplication).
  \item \texttt{rescaling\_identity} carries \emph{no} hypotheses and states exactly the rearrangement
    $a=b+(a-b)$; \texttt{unfold; ring} is honest for it.
  \item The cubic remainder bound $|s_t(u)|\le C\|u\|^3/\sqrt t$ is named only in the documentation as a
    later-assumed estimate; the file contains no theorem claiming to prove it, matching the informal
    description (the primer's GPT-5.5-Pro Phase-1 strategy takes such bounds as explicit hypotheses).
\end{itemize}

\section{Simplifications applied}
\textbf{One landed: a redundant import.} The file imported \texttt{Mathlib.Topology.Algebra.Module.Basic}
explicitly, but \texttt{Laplace.Multi.Basic} already imports
\texttt{Mathlib.Analysis.InnerProductSpace.EuclideanDist}, which transitively supplies the
\texttt{ContinuousLinearMap} ($\to_L[\mathbb R]$) notation and instances the file uses. Removed under
\textbf{rebuild-arbitration}: this is the dead-import class, and --- per the standing caution that a
symbol-grep is \emph{not} a valid dead-import test --- the rebuild is the only honest arbiter. A
load-bearing import, removed, would fail elaboration immediately; instead the file rebuilt green ($2544$
jobs) with all five declarations byte-identical. GPT-affirmed and fast-forward-merged.

GPT's two other proposals were declined. One was tactic-golf (replace the identity's \texttt{unfold; ring}
with a \texttt{sub\_add\_cancel}-based \texttt{simpa}). The other was a docstring rewording --- ``definitional''
$\to$ ``immediate algebraic rearrangement'', and softening the cubic bound to ``later assumed''. Unlike the
\reviewref[``2D mirror'' fix]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-multi-affine-bilinear-cov} in the sibling file, this
was \emph{not} correcting an inaccuracy: the identity genuinely is a definitional rearrangement, and the file
already labels the cubic bound a downstream hypothesis in its own setup paragraph. Churning accurate prose is
not a simplification, so it was left as written.

\section{Disagreements and open questions}
None on the mathematics.

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{A redundant import is the dead-open's cousin --- same rebuild-arbitration, same caution.} The
    transitive-availability argument (here, $\to_L[\mathbb R]$ via \texttt{EuclideanDist}) is a strong
    \emph{prior}, not a proof; the rebuild is what settles it, because a removed load-bearing import fails
    instantly and unmistakably. Both the static reasoning and the independent reviewer agreed before the build
    confirmed.
  \item \textbf{Not every reviewer docstring suggestion is a fix.} The discriminator that landed the
    sibling's ``2D mirror'' edit but declined this file's rewording is whether the existing prose is
    \emph{inaccurate} (objective, landable) versus merely \emph{rephraseable} (stylistic, decline). ``The
    identity is definitional'' is true; leave it.
\end{itemize}
