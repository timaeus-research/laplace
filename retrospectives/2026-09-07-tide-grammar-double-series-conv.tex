\begin{abstract}
The Cauchy product of two double power series, on coefficient arrays $\mathbb N^2\to\mathbb R$:
$(f*g)_k=\sum_{a\le k}f_ag_{k-a}$, with the weighted $\ell^1$ norm $N_\rho(f)=\sum_k|f_k|\rho^{|k|}$
and weighted summability. The two results (Astra~\#7(a)) are the closure
$N_\rho(f*g)\le N_\rho(f)N_\rho(g)$ (with weighted summability of the product) and multiplicativity
of evaluation $\sum_k(f*g)_ku^iv^j=(\sum_kf_ku^iv^j)(\sum_kg_ku^iv^j)$ for $|u|,|v|\le\rho$. Both come from
one finite-fibre bridge: the fibre of the addition map $(\mathbb N^2)^2\to\mathbb N^2$ over $k$ is
in bijection with the box $\{a\le k\}$, so \texttt{HasSum.tsum\_fiberwise} regroups the product
family. No \texttt{HasAntidiagonal} instance on $\mathbb N^2$ is needed. Module~1 of the
coefficient closure for $\eta e^{\beta s\xi}$. Zero \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{axiom}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Mathematics}

The weighted product family $H(a,b)=(|f_a|\rho^{|a|})(|g_b|\rho^{|b|})$ is absolutely summable
with sum $N_\rho(f)N_\rho(g)$ (\texttt{summable\_mul\_of\_summable\_norm},
\texttt{tsum\_mul\_tsum\_of\_summable\_norm}). Grouping it along the fibres of addition
(\texttt{HasSum.tsum\_fiberwise}) and converting each fibre sum to the finite sum over the box gives
$B_k=\rho^{|k|}\sum_{a\le k}|f_a||g_{k-a}|$ with $\sum_kB_k=N_\rho(f)N_\rho(g)$, and
$|(f*g)_k|\rho^{|k|}\le B_k$ by the triangle inequality; the weight identity is
$\rho^{|a|}\rho^{|k-a|}=\rho^{|k|}$. Evaluation multiplicativity is the same grouping applied to
the signed family $(f_au^{a_1}v^{a_2})(g_bu^{b_1}v^{b_2})$, absolutely summable because
$|u^iv^j|\le\rho^{i+j}$, with $u^{a_1}u^{(k-a)_1}=u^{k_1}$ on each fibre. As Astra~\#7 stressed,
the weighted family is grouped, never the unweighted one: weighted summability at $\rho<1$ does not
give unweighted summability.

\section{Paper-facing meaning}

This is the algebra needed to build the coefficient array of $\eta(u,v)e^{\beta s\xi(u,v)}$ from the
double series of $\xi$ and $\eta$ (next units: convolution powers, the exponential series and its
majorant, then the amplitude adapter feeding \texttt{twoD\_cutoff\_analytic}), so that the analytic
$d=2$ cutoff theorem applies to the chart amplitude of \texttt{thm:TaylorTree} under the paper's
analyticity hypothesis on $\xi,\eta$ alone.

\section{Lean notes}

\begin{itemize}
\item Avoid antidiagonals on $\mathbb N^2$ altogether: index the convolution by the box
$\{a\le k\}$ with componentwise truncated subtraction, and prove one \texttt{Equiv} between the
box and the addition fibre; \texttt{Equiv.tsum\_eq} plus \texttt{Finset.tsum\_subtype} then give the
fibre-sum bridge.
\item After \texttt{rw [← e.tsum\_eq]} the summand is \texttt{H (e c)}; a \texttt{change} to the
unfolded pair is needed before \texttt{Finset.tsum\_subtype} matches.
\item For a subtype of a product, \texttt{p.1} is the value, not its first component; write
\texttt{(p : (ℕ × ℕ) × (ℕ × ℕ)).1}.
\item \texttt{Real.norm\_of\_nonneg} with an explicit \texttt{positivity} proof converts norms of
weighted absolute values; \texttt{simp} with \texttt{Real.norm\_eq\_abs} tends to reassociate.
\item Rewriting \texttt{k} via \texttt{a + psub k a = k} also rewrites inside \texttt{psub k a};
derive component identities with \texttt{omega} from \texttt{mem\_box} instead.
\end{itemize}
