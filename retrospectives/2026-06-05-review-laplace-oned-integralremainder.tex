\begin{abstract}
A soundness review of \texttt{Laplace/OneD/IntegralRemainder.lean} --- at 2668 lines the largest
file in the laplace seabed, and the analytic foundation on which the whole 1D anharmonic programme
rests: the $I_n/J_n$ integral-remainder asymptotics culminating in the SLT primer's Stage-2
covariance formula $\mathrm{Cov}_t[w^2,w]=-2\alpha/(\lambda^3t^2)+o(t^{-2})$ (eq.\ 4.10). Result: a
\textbf{clean fidelity pass on every headline}, with ten landed edits --- two dead opens, four
docstring corrections, and four dead locals --- and several larger refactors deliberately left as
forward tides. Reviewing it completes independent coverage of the laplace \texttt{OneD} directory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
The capstone of a session-long sweep of the laplace \texttt{OneD} directory. Almost every other
\texttt{OneD} file --- the anharmonic cumulant arc, the harmonic stack, the bounded-prior pair ---
ultimately consumes the asymptotics proved here. The file is the bridge from the elementary Gaussian
moments of Stage~1 to the first genuine payoff of Stage~2.

\section{What was reviewed}
The headline chain: a pointwise Taylor-remainder bound for $e^{-s}-(1-s)$ against the Gaussian weight;
polynomial$\times$Gaussian integrability; the linearised integrand decomposition; the $J_n$
first-order expansions $J_n=M_n-(A/\sqrt t)M_{n+3}-(B/t)M_{n+4}+O(1/t)$ with Gaussian moments
$M_{2k}=(2k-1)!!\sqrt{2\pi}$; the $I_n\!\leftrightarrow\!J_n$ substitution identity
$\sqrt{\lambda t}^{\,n+1}I_n=J_n$; the $I_n$ asymptotics; \texttt{cov\_coefficient\_identity}; and the
assembled \texttt{cov\_anharmonic\_asymptotic}, \texttt{mean\_anharmonic\_asymptotic},
\texttt{var\_anharmonic\_J\_form\_asymptotic}, \texttt{cov\_self\_anharmonic\_asymptotic}, and the
explicit $O(t^{-2})$ rate theorems. Ground truth: the primer's eq.\ 4.10 and the file's own
docstrings, plus internal consistency of the moment/substitution algebra.

\section{Fidelity / soundness findings}
\textbf{Sound; no defect in any headline.} The reviewer verified, item by item: the covariance
coefficient $-2\alpha/\lambda^3$ (sign and power $t^2$ correct); the mean $-\alpha/(2\lambda^2)$; the
variance and self-covariance $1/\lambda$; both explicit $O(t^{-2})$ rate statements; the Gaussian
normalisations $\sqrt{2\pi},3\sqrt{2\pi},15\sqrt{2\pi}$ and the vanishing odd moments; the $J_1,J_3$
constants $-3A\sqrt{2\pi},-15A\sqrt{2\pi}$; and the substitution identity's Jacobian/power. ``No
defect found'' anywhere in the constants, signs, exponents, or the Jacobian.

\textbf{One honest under-statement (report-only).} The specialised \texttt{J\_0}/\texttt{J\_2} and
\texttt{I\_0}/\texttt{I\_2} theorems state only the leading term plus $O(1/t)$, absorbing the
explicit quartic first-correction coefficient that \texttt{J\_n\_asymptotic} could expose. These are
true as written; \emph{strengthening} them to surface the coefficient is a stronger statement --- an
API change, hence a forward tide, not a review edit.

\section{Simplifications and documentation fixes applied}
\textbf{Two dead opens.} \texttt{open Real} and \texttt{open Set} both carried nothing --- every
\texttt{Real.*} and every interval (\texttt{Set.Ioi}/\texttt{Set.Iic}) is qualified; the bare
$\exp$/$\sqrt{}$/$\pi$ tokens live only in comments. \texttt{open MeasureTheory} (166 bare $\int$) and
\texttt{open scoped Nat} (the $!!$) stay.

\textbf{Four docstring corrections --- the file had outgrown its own header.} The module docstring
still described only the opening pointwise bound and asserted ``no new analytic content beyond the
algebra,'' for a file that in fact proves the entire covariance result; it was retitled and the false
claim corrected. A section header ``\texttt{Specialised asymptotics for n = 1}'' actually covers
$J_0$--$J_3$. A summary block claimed the covariance assembly was ``left as a follow-up'' --- it is
proved later in the very same file. And the \texttt{cov\_anharmonic\_J\_form\_asymptotic} docstring
self-contradicted (``multiply by $\lambda^{3/2}$'' then ``dividing by $\lambda^{3/2}$''); the proof
divides (\texttt{div\_const}), so the prose was fixed to ``divides'' and clarified that the theorem
states the $J_n$-quotient limit.

\textbf{Four dead locals.} Three unused \texttt{have}s in \texttt{J\_0\_asymptotic}
(\texttt{habs}, \texttt{hpos\_bound}, \texttt{h\_quartic\_abs} --- each defined then never referenced;
the calc that follows uses none) and one unused \texttt{with hM\_def} equation name on a \texttt{set}.
All ten edits build green ($8254$ jobs), no new in-file warnings, exported signatures byte-identical;
the reviewer affirmed each.

\section{Disagreements and open questions}
None substantive. The \texttt{import Laplace.OneD.Anharmonic} is transitively redundant (its
\texttt{anharmonicPotential} is re-exported via \texttt{Rescaling}, which imports \texttt{Anharmonic}
directly), but it was \emph{kept}: \texttt{anharmonicPotential} is used directly and pervasively here,
so the import is a heavily-used \emph{direct} dependency that merely happens to be reachable two ways
--- removing it would make a real dependency implicit (a clarity-reducing refactor), not delete dead
code. The reviewer agreed it was ``not clearly dead.''

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{The largest files drift the most in their headers, and the module docstring drifts
    first.} A file that grows section-by-section over many tides keeps the docstring it was born with:
    here a 2668-line covariance proof still announced itself as a pointwise-bound lemma with ``no new
    analytic content.'' On a big mature file, budget the fidelity check to (i) the handful of headline
    \emph{statements} and (ii) the \emph{navigational} docstrings (module header, section headers,
    summary blocks) --- those, not the routine integrability lemmas, are where the drift hides.
  \item \textbf{``Redundant import'' $\ne$ ``dead import'' when the module's symbols are used
    directly.} The dead-import test is ``is any symbol of this module used here?'', not ``is this
    module reachable another way?''. \texttt{Anharmonic} fails the second but passes the first
    emphatically --- keep it. (Contrast a genuinely symbol-less import, which the build lets you
    drop.)
  \item \textbf{A self-contradicting docstring is an unambiguous fix.} ``Multiply by $\lambda^{3/2}$''
    and ``divide by $\lambda^{3/2}$'' cannot both be right; the proof term (\texttt{div\_const})
    breaks the tie. Internal contradiction is the easiest docstring defect to adjudicate --- no
    external ground truth needed.
\end{itemize}

    reviewed.
\end{itemize}
