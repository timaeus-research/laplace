\begin{abstract}
For a nonnegative kernel $g$ the product-kernel block
$J(t)=\int\!\!\int_{(0,b]^2}u^{h_1}v^{h_2}g(tu^{k_1}v^{k_2})\,du\,dv$ is bounded explicitly rather
than asymptotically (Astra~\#5(d)). With equal exponents $p$ and $R=b^{k_1+k_2}$ the exact density is
$(k_1k_2)^{-1}r^{p-1}\log(R/r)$, and for $t\ge1$
\[
J(t)\le\frac{t^{-p}}{k_1k_2}\bigl(\log t\cdot M_p(g)+U_{p,R}(g)\bigr),\qquad
M_p=\int_0^\infty s^{p-1}g,\quad U_{p,R}=\int_0^\infty s^{p-1}\log_+(R/s)\,g;
\]
for bounded $0\le g\le G_0$, $J(t)\le G_0\,b^{h_1+1}b^{h_2+1}/((h_1+1)(h_2+1))$ for every $t$. These
constants replace the \texttt{IsBigO} mixed-remainder bound and are what the strict-gap parameter
wrapper (unit~99) needs as an integrable $K(w)$. Zero \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{axiom}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Mathematics}

$J(t)$ is the block integral of unit~86 with $\beta=0$ and the constant amplitude $g(s)$, so the
one-variable density is $k_2^{-1}r^{p-1}g(tr)\int_\varepsilon^bu^{-1}du$ with
$\varepsilon=(r/b^{k_2})^{1/k_1}$, and $\log b-\log\varepsilon=k_1^{-1}\log(R/r)$. Substituting
$x=tr$ (\texttt{integral\_Ioc\_rpow\_mul\_comp\_mul\_left}),
$J(t)=\frac{t^{-p}}{k_1k_2}\int_0^{tR}x^{p-1}\bigl(\log t+\log(R/x)\bigr)g(x)\,dx$, and on $(0,tR]$
the bracket lies between $0$ and $\log t+\log_+(R/x)$ (lower bound from $x\le tR$); extending the
integral to $(0,\infty)$ over the nonnegative dominating integrand gives the bound. The bounded
kernel estimate is Fubini for the product of the two monomial integrals on the product box.

\section{Paper-facing meaning}

The mixed remainder of the rectangular decomposition is dominated by
$J(t)$ with $g(s)=e^{-\beta s^2/2+\beta Ls}(1+s)^D$-type kernels and shifted exponents; the explicit
bound makes the remainder term of the $d=2$ cutoff theorem quantitative, which is the prerequisite
for instantiating the strict-gap parameter wrapper on mixed critical/noncritical blocks (the
constants must be integrable in the parameter $w$, which pointwise \texttt{IsBigO} does not give).
The unequal-exponent and gap-uniform bounds of Astra~\#5(d) are the natural follow-up.

\section{Lean notes}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{setIntegral\_congr\_fun} on the density hands the amplitude as an unreduced
\texttt{(fun x s => g s) u (t * r)}; \texttt{beta\_reduce} before rewriting with
\texttt{integral\_mul\_const}.
\item Lower bounds for $\log(R/x)$ on $(0,tR]$: go through
\texttt{Real.log\_le\_log hx0 hx.2} and \texttt{Real.log\_mul}, not through inverses and divisions.
\item Continuity of a parametric integral in the outer variable is expensive to prove directly;
integrating over the product box (\texttt{twoDAmp\_eq\_prod}, \texttt{integral\_mono\_ae},
\texttt{integral\_prod\_mul}) avoids it entirely.
\end{itemize}
