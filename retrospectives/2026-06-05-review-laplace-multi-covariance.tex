\begin{abstract}
A soundness review of \texttt{Laplace/Multi/Covariance.lean} (3998 lines): the Stage-3 multivariate
Laplace covariance theorem \texttt{lem:laplace\_cov}, whose headline
\texttt{gibbsCov\_first\_order\_rate\_weak} establishes
$\mathrm{Cov}_t[\varphi,\psi]=\tfrac1t\langle a,\Sigma b\rangle+o(1/t)$ (weak track, $\Sigma=H^{-1}$).
Result: a clean fidelity pass at the statement level; the landables were two dead opens, three stale
docstrings, and one dead private lemma. Two of the docstring fixes are the interesting part --- a
header that advertised the \emph{sharp} rate this file does not prove, and a lemma docstring that
claimed ``internal sorries'' in a proof that has none.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
The second of the large \texttt{Multi/} analytic files, built on
\reviewref[RescaledIntegrals]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-multi-rescaledintegrals}, and the weak-track
half of the multivariate covariance result (the sharp-track half is
\texttt{gibbsCov\_first\_order\_rate\_sharp} in \texttt{CovarianceSharp.lean}). Roughly 2600 of its
3998 lines are private asymptotic-integral infrastructure between the public headlines.

\section{What was reviewed}
The five public headlines --- the partition asymptotic
\texttt{rescaledPartition\_eq\_gaussianZ\_add\_O\_inv\_sqrt} and its lower bound, the single-observable
bound, the pair expectation \texttt{rescaledExpectation\_pair\_eq\_main\_add\_O\_inv\_sqrt}, and
\texttt{gibbsCov\_first\_order\_rate\_weak} --- checked for the $1/t$ rate, the $\langle
a,H^{-1}b\rangle$ main contraction, the Gaussian sign, and the error orders.

\section{Fidelity / soundness findings}
\textbf{Sound at the statement level.} The reviewer confirmed every headline: the main term contracts
through $H^{-1}$ (not $H$), the Gaussian pair term has the right sign, and the scaled errors are
$O(t^{-1/2})$ (so $\mathrm{Cov}_t=\text{main}/t+O(t^{-3/2})=o(1/t)$). ``No headline-level $H$ vs
$H^{-1}$ mistake, no sign error.''

\textbf{Three stale/over-claiming docstrings, two of them notable.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \emph{The header advertised the sharp result.} The module header stated
    $\mathrm{Cov}_t=\tfrac1t\langle a,\Sigma b\rangle+O(t^{-2})$ and said the file ``(eventually)
    proves'' the explicit $\le K/t$ bound --- but the public theorem is named
    \texttt{\_weak} and proves $\le K/\sqrt t$. The sharp $O(t^{-2})$ result is a \emph{different,
    deliberately separate} theorem (\texttt{gibbsCov\_first\_order\_rate\_sharp} in
    \texttt{CovarianceSharp}, whose own header cross-references back here). So this is not an
    under-proven theorem to escalate --- it is a header describing the wrong file's result. Fixed to
    state the weak form and cross-reference the sharp track. (The first attempt fixed the body
    paragraph but left the opening line still reading $O(t^{-2})$; the affirmation pass caught that, and
    a second pass corrected the opening line to $o(1/t)$.)
  \item \emph{A docstring claimed sorries that do not exist.} The private
    \texttt{abs\_integral\_dot\_mul\_rescaled\_weight\_correction\_le} was titled ``(in-progress,
    contains internal sorries)'' and said two integrability facts ``remain as internal sorries'' --- but
    a grep, a green build, and \texttt{scripts/sorries} all confirm the file has \emph{zero} sorries.
    The proof was completed and the docstring never updated; a reader would wrongly think the result
    unsound. Corrected to state the (completed) derivation.
  \item The headline's own docstring still said ``statement only / proof in progress'' (it is proved)
    and pointed at a non-existent filename \texttt{Covariance.Sharp.lean}; both corrected.
\end{itemize}

\section{Simplifications applied}
\textbf{Two dead opens and a dead private lemma.} \texttt{open Module} was fully dead (no
\texttt{Module.*}/\texttt{finrank} anywhere); the section-local \texttt{open MeasureTheory} was
redundant under the namespace-level open (the same pattern as RescaledIntegrals). And the private
\texttt{integrable\_dot\_dot\_mul\_rescaled\_weight\_correction} had no call sites --- the bilinear
correction re-proves its integrability inline --- so it was removed (private, so the public surface is
untouched). Build green, public signatures byte-identical.

\section{Disagreements and open questions}
None left open. The affirmation pass rejected the first header fix as incomplete (the opening line
still advertised $O(t^{-2})$); the second pass resolved it. Several further cleanups are report-only
for a hygiene tide: the \texttt{*\_def} dead equation-names and dead-local constant blocks (on this
\texttt{positivity}/\texttt{field\_simp}-heavy file they carry the implicit-consumption risk, so they
want individual build-arbitration), and a few unused \texttt{hGauss} parameters on private lemmas
(a signature change of private decls, multi-site, low value).

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{A header can advertise a sibling file's result.} When a result is split into a
    weak-track and a sharp-track file, the weak file's header is prone to stating the \emph{sharp}
    theorem as its own headline. The tell: the public theorem is named \texttt{\_weak} but the prose
    claims the sharp rate. The fix is to scope the header to what the file proves and cross-reference
    the other track --- not to escalate (the theorem is complete, just mislabelled).
  \item \textbf{``Contains internal sorries'' is a verifiable claim --- verify it.} A docstring
    advertising sorries in a file that has none is worse than a mere staleness nit: it impugns a sound
    result. \texttt{grep} $+$ green build $+$ \texttt{scripts/sorries} settle it in seconds; here all
    three agreed the lemma is complete.
  \item \textbf{The affirmation pass catches half-done doc fixes.} My header fix corrected the body but
    left the opening line over-claiming; sending the diff back caught the residue. A docstring fix is
    not done until \emph{every} line of the docstring matches the theorem.
\end{itemize}

\section{Follow-ups}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]

    private \texttt{hGauss} parameters.
  \item The last never-reviewed laplace files are \texttt{CovarianceSharp} (4179L) and

\end{itemize}
