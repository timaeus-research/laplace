\begin{abstract}
A soundness review of \texttt{Laplace/Gibbs.lean}: the seabed's foundational definitions ---
\texttt{partitionFunction}, \texttt{gibbsExpectation}, \texttt{gibbsCov} --- and the bilinearity /
scalar-pull / constant-collapse algebra that every downstream file uses to manipulate Gibbs
expectations without unfolding. Result: a \textbf{clean fidelity pass with two edits} (a dead
\texttt{open} and one over-specified header sentence). For a file this central, the value of the
review is the confirmation itself: the objects the whole programme is built on say exactly what the
primer means.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
This is the root of the laplace dependency graph: $Z(t)$, $\langle\cdot\rangle_t$, and
$\mathrm{Cov}_t[\cdot,\cdot]$ (with prior $\pi\equiv 1$), plus the algebraic lemmas
(\texttt{gibbsExpectation\_\{const,smul,zero,add\}}, \texttt{gibbsCov\_\{symm,smul,const,add,zero\}}
in both slots) that the affine- and multilinear-observable covariance results across the seabed call.
Every other laplace file imports it. It had never been independently reviewed.

\section{What was reviewed}
The three definitions against the primer's $Z/\langle\cdot\rangle/\mathrm{Cov}$ with $\pi\equiv 1$;
and each algebraic lemma's \emph{hypotheses} --- the crux for a definitions file, since the interest
is precisely which facts need $Z(t)\ne 0$, which need integrability, and which are unconditional.

\section{Fidelity / soundness findings}
\textbf{Sound; no defect.} The reviewer confirmed all three definitions faithful (including the
implicit Lebesgue measure realising $\pi\equiv 1$) and \emph{no sign error} in the covariance
$\langle\varphi\psi\rangle-\langle\varphi\rangle\langle\psi\rangle$. The hypothesis placement is
exactly right: \texttt{gibbsExpectation\_const} genuinely needs $Z\ne 0$ (else Lean's totalised
$\,/\,0=0$ gives $\langle c\rangle=0\ne c$); the \texttt{\_smul}/\texttt{\_zero} and all the
\texttt{gibbsCov} constant/scalar/zero lemmas are correctly \emph{unconditional}, their docstrings
correctly noting that at $Z=0$ both sides collapse to $0$; and the additivity lemmas carry exactly the
integrability hypotheses \texttt{integral\_add} requires, with no spurious $Z\ne 0$. The lone textual
mismatch: the module header described the setting as a ``smooth potential $L$'' with ``$t>0$,'' while
the definitions and lemmas are stated for arbitrary $L:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ and arbitrary
$t:\mathbb R$ --- the code is strictly more general than its own prose.

\section{Simplifications and documentation fixes applied}
\textbf{One dead open, one prose correction.} \texttt{open Real} carried nothing (every
\texttt{Real.exp} is qualified; the only bare \texttt{exp} tokens are in the docstring);
\texttt{open MeasureTheory} stays (bare \texttt{Integrable}, \texttt{integral\_const\_mul},
\texttt{integral\_add}). And the header was relaxed to state that smoothness of $L$ and positivity of
$t$ belong to the primer's setting, not to the generality of the file's definitions. Build green, no
warnings, signatures byte-identical; the reviewer affirmed both. No dead locals, no unnecessary
hypotheses, no unused imports were found --- a tight foundational file.

\section{Disagreements and open questions}
None. (One terseness note left report-only: \texttt{gibbsCov\_add\_right}'s docstring, unlike
\texttt{\_add\_left}'s, does not restate the integrability hypotheses --- accurate as written, since
the hypotheses are in the signature.)

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{For a definitions file, the review \emph{is} the hypothesis audit.} There is no
    asymptotic constant to re-derive; the entire fidelity question is ``does each lemma demand exactly
    the hypotheses it needs --- $Z\ne 0$ where a division must be inverted, integrability where
    \texttt{integral\_add} fires, nothing where the totalised $/0=0$ makes the statement unconditional
    --- and does the docstring's $Z=0$ reasoning hold up?'' Confirming that for the root objects is
    high-value precisely because everything downstream inherits it.
  \item \textbf{Header prose over-specifies more often than it under-specifies, on foundational
    files.} A definitions file is written to serve a motivating application (here: smooth $L$,
    $t>0$), and the header tends to inherit that framing even though the Lean objects are deliberately
    general. Worth a glance: do the stated ambient hypotheses actually appear in any signature?
\end{itemize}
