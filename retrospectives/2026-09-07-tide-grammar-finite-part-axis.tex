\begin{abstract}
For $f(v)=\sum_{m<M}f_mv^m+R_M(v)$ on $(0,b]$ with $|R_M(v)|\le Hv^M$ and $0\le\gamma<M$, the weighted
axis integral $J(\varepsilon)=\int_\varepsilon^bv^{-1-\gamma}f(v)\,dv$ has the exact expansion
\[
J(\varepsilon)=\mathrm{FP}_\gamma f-\sum_{m<M}f_m\,\mathrm{axisPrim}(\gamma,\varepsilon,m)+E(\varepsilon),
\qquad |E(\varepsilon)|\le\frac{H}{M-\gamma}\varepsilon^{M-\gamma},
\]
where $\mathrm{axisPrim}(\gamma,x,m)=\log x$ at the resonance $m=\gamma$ and $x^{m-\gamma}/(m-\gamma)$
otherwise, and the finite part $\mathrm{FP}_\gamma f=\int_0^bv^{-1-\gamma}(f-T_Mf)\,dv+\sum_{m<M}f_m\,
\mathrm{axisPrim}(\gamma,b,m)$ collects the regularised integral and the upper-endpoint counterterms.
With $\varepsilon=(r/A)^{1/k}$ this produces the shifted exponents $(m-\gamma)/k$ and the resonant
$\log(A/r)/k$ of the $d=2$ density expansion. This is Astra~\#4's rank-2 unit, the gateway to the
$\tau$-cutoff $d=2$ theorem. Zero \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{axiom}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Mathematics}

On $(\varepsilon,b]$, $v^{-1-\gamma}f=v^{-1-\gamma}R_M+\sum_{m<M}f_mv^{m-1-\gamma}$, and the
monomials integrate exactly to $\mathrm{axisPrim}(\gamma,b,m)-\mathrm{axisPrim}(\gamma,\varepsilon,m)$
(Mathlib's \texttt{integral\_rpow} off the origin, and \texttt{integral\_inv} at the resonance
$m-1-\gamma=-1$). The remainder term is integrable down to $0$ because
$v^{-1-\gamma}|R_M|\le Hv^{M-1-\gamma}$ with $M-1-\gamma>-1$; splitting at $\varepsilon$ gives the
regularised integral over $(0,b]$ minus a tail over $(0,\varepsilon]$, and the tail is bounded by
$H\int_0^\varepsilon v^{M-1-\gamma}=H\varepsilon^{M-\gamma}/(M-\gamma)$.

As Astra~\#4 stressed, the regularised integral alone is not the constant coefficient: the
upper-endpoint counterterms $\sum_mf_m\,\mathrm{axisPrim}(\gamma,b,m)$ are part of
$\mathrm{FP}_\gamma f$. The formalisation subtracts the full order-$M$ Taylor polynomial (rather
than only the terms with $m\le\lfloor\gamma\rfloor$); the resulting finite part is independent of
$M>\gamma$ because the extra monomials contribute exactly cancelling terms, but that independence is
not needed for the expansion and is left for later.

\section{Paper-facing meaning}

In the $d=2$ block, a face term $u^ia_i(v,s)$ has density
$k_1^{-1}r^{p+i/k_1-1}\int_{(r/A)^{1/k_2}}^{b_2}v^{-1-k_2i/k_1}a_i(v,s)\,dv$; this lemma with
$\gamma=k_2i/k_1$ and $\varepsilon=(r/A)^{1/k_2}$ (\texttt{axisPrim\_rpow}) yields the exponents
$p+i/k_1+(m-\gamma)/k_2=p+m/k_2$, a $\log(A/r)$ precisely at the collision $i/k_1=m/k_2$, and the
finite-part coefficient $D_q(s)$ of the cutoff density theorem. It generalises the unit-87 axis
integral $I_\Psi=\int_0^b(\Psi-\Psi(0))/u$, which is the case $\gamma=0$, $M=1$.

\section{Lean notes}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{Real.rpow\_neg\_one} does not exist; the resonant case converts $v^{-1}$ to $v^{-1}$
via \texttt{Real.rpow\_neg} and \texttt{Real.rpow\_one} inside an
\texttt{intervalIntegral.integral\_congr}.
\item \texttt{integral\_rpow} needs \texttt{Or.inr ⟨hc, h0⟩} with \texttt{0 ∉ uIcc ε b} when the
exponent may be below $-1$; \texttt{uIcc\_of\_le} turns \texttt{uIcc} into \texttt{Icc}.
\item \texttt{← intervalIntegral.integral\_of\_le} in a hypothesis with two set integrals rewrites
the first one; compute the power integral in a separate \texttt{have} first.
\item Membership in \texttt{Icc ε b} vs \texttt{Ioc ε b}: \texttt{ContinuousOn.integrableOn\_Icc}
hands \texttt{ε ≤ v}, the pointwise decomposition hands \texttt{ε < v}; \texttt{hε.trans} vs
\texttt{lt\_of\_lt\_of\_le} accordingly.
\item \texttt{Finset.measurable\_sum \_ fun m \_ => measurable\_const.mul (measurable\_id.pow\_const m)}
for measurability of a polynomial.
\end{itemize}
