\begin{abstract}
\noindent The germbij note's multivariate Hessian step, as stated:
for a loss carrying the H4 domain package, the $U$-restricted
posterior at inverse temperature $q^{-2}$ satisfies
$q^{-1}\,\mathbb{E}_q[w_i] \to 0$ and
$q^{-2}\,\mathbb{E}_q[w_i w_j] \to (H^{-1})_{ij}$, hence
\[
q^{-2}\,\mathrm{Cov}_q(w_i, w_j) \;\longrightarrow\; (H^{-1})_{ij}.
\]
The tide is pure bookkeeping, exactly as staged: one dilation
identity factors every posterior integral into
$q^d e^{-L(0)/q^2}$ times an H4 rescaled integrand --- an exact
identity at each $q$, so the prefactors cancel in the normalized
ratios algebraically, not asymptotically --- and the H4 limits then
divide. The scoping consult's warning that the first-moment limit is
load-bearing (covariance is not the raw second moment merely because
the minimum sits at the origin) is visible in the final proof shape:
the covariance limit is `second minus first-times-first', with the
vanishing first moments doing real work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

Stages H2--H4 built the quadratic Gaussian package, the local
quadratic approximation, and the generic dominated convergence. What
remained between those theorems and the germbij sentence ``$t\,
\mathrm{Cov}_t[w_i, w_j] \to (H^{-1})_{ij}$, recovering $H$'' was the
change of variables between the posterior's own integrals and the
rescaled integrands, and the division bookkeeping.

\section{The thing formalised}

For \texttt{posteriorIntegral}$(f, q) = \int \mathbf{1}_U(w) f(w)
e^{-L(w)/q^2} dw$: the dilation identity
\[
\mathrm{posteriorIntegral}(f, q)
= q^d\, e^{-L(0)/q^2} \int \mathbf{1}_U(qx)\, f(qx)\,
e^{-(L(qx) - L(0))/q^2}\, dx
\qquad (q > 0),
\]
the $q$-power extraction for coordinate observables, and the three
limits above, ending in \texttt{tendsto\_normalized\_covariance}.

\section{How the proof works}

The dilation wrapper from the programme's opening tide gives the
substitution $w = qx$; the indicator transports to the preimage set,
and the Boltzmann factor splits off $e^{-L(0)/q^2}$ by one
exponential identity that \texttt{ring} proves formally in
$(q^2)^{-1}$ (valid without any $q \neq 0$ hypothesis). Coordinate
observables pull their $q$-powers through the indicator by constant
multiplication. Because the identity is exact at every $q$, the
normalized moments equal ratios of H4 integrand integrals on the
eventual set where the zeroth integral is positive (guaranteed by
its limit $\mathrm{jacInv}\,H \cdot Z_0 > 0$), and
\texttt{Tendsto.div} with a nonzero limiting denominator finishes
each moment. The covariance limit is then
$H^{-1}_{ij} - 0 \cdot 0$.

\section{Where it stalled and how it moved}

No stalls; three quick diagnostic passes, every repair from the
catalogue: \texttt{Tendsto.div} produces a \texttt{Pi.div} of
lambdas that \texttt{field\_simp} cannot see through
(\texttt{simp only [Pi.div\_apply]} first); \texttt{field\_simp}
needs the \texttt{jacInv} nonzeroness in scope by name; a rewrite
under an unapplied lambda needed a \texttt{simp only []}
beta-reduction; and three trailing \texttt{ring}s died after
\texttt{field\_simp} closed their goals. One repository hygiene find:
a \texttt{texput.log} accidentally committed in April surfaced when a
stray \texttt{pdflatex} touched it; removed on this branch.

\section{Mathlib lookup versus new mathematics}

Essentially no new Mathlib surface: the tide consumes the
programme's own interfaces (the dilation wrapper, the H4 integrand
and its three limits) plus \texttt{Set.indicator\_const\_mul} and
the filter algebra of \texttt{Tendsto}. New (project-level): the
posterior-to-rescaled dictionary and the three normalized limits in
posterior language.

\section{What this opens}

Stage H6 --- the last of the commissioned milestone --- is now a
handful of lines: two losses whose covariance data differ by
$o(q^2)$ have $H_1^{-1} = H_2^{-1}$ by uniqueness of limits, hence
$H_1 = H_2$ by invertibility. After it: the germbij note's
multivariate margin markers, and the further multivariate
commissions (degree-$k$ tensor recovery, degenerate strata) as
future scoping consults.
