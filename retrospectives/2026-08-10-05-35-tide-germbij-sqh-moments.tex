\begin{abstract}
The germbij note recovers the moments $M_\alpha$ of a
quasi-homogeneous model from expansion coefficients by a
linear-independence argument over partial derivatives of delta
distributions, with care at exponent collisions. The scoping consult
found the argument dissolves under a change of observable: testing
with the monomial $x^\alpha$ itself, anisotropic scaling gives
\[
  t^{\langle q, \alpha\rangle}\,
  \big\langle x^\alpha \big\rangle_t
  \;=\; \frac{M_\alpha}{M_0}
  \qquad\text{exactly, for every } t > 0,
\]
and exponent collisions between different multi-indices never enter
a single observable's coefficient --- so no distribution theory is
needed, and the identity is exact rather than asymptotic. This tide
formalises the identity for every multi-index (including odd ones,
extending the even-coordinate recovery perimeter) and the comparison
form: two quasi-homogeneous models with the same weights whose
normalized monomial moments agree at a single temperature have equal
moment ratios.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

After the forward-expansion programme closed the nondegenerate case
of Theorem~3.1 in both directions, the remaining debt is constructive
class (c). The scoping consult ranked its three pieces: the
$M_\alpha$ recovery (this tide, smallest delta), the one-grade
weighted comparison (medium), and the coarea/Gelfand--Leray layer
(blocked on Mathlib geometric measure theory). It also fixed the
sensible endpoint --- principal-model moment ratios plus conditional
grade recovery --- short of the full analytic weighted induction,
which is not tide-shaped. A parallel session holds the inverse-half
perimeter; the ledger race-check confirmed no overlap.
