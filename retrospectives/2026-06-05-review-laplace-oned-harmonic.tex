\begin{abstract}
A soundness review of \texttt{Laplace/OneD/Harmonic.lean}: the harmonic Gibbs measure --- partition function
$Z_\lambda(t)=\sqrt{2\pi/(\lambda t)}$, the moments $\langle x^{2k}\rangle_t=(2k-1)!!/(\lambda t)^k$ and
$\langle x^{2k+1}\rangle_t=0$, and $\mathrm{Cov}_t[x^2,x]=0$. Result: \textbf{a clean fidelity pass with one
landed edit (a dead \texttt{open Set}) and one instructive non-edit} --- a mathematically-dead proof block
that turned out to be load-bearing for the \texttt{unusedVariables} linter, reverted once the rebuild exposed
it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
The harmonic case is the leading-order Laplace computation and the base the anharmonic programme perturbs
around (\texttt{harmonicPotential} is what the \reviewref[anharmonic base]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-oned-anharmonic}
decomposes against). Its moments reduce directly to the
\reviewref[Gaussian-moment lemmas]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-oned-gaussian-moments} by setting the inverse
covariance to $\lambda t$.

\section{What was reviewed}
A definition, a private integrand-rewrite helper, and six theorems: the even/odd numerator integrals, the
partition function, the even/odd Gibbs expectations, and the harmonic covariance $\mathrm{Cov}_t[x^2,x]=0$.

\section{Fidelity / soundness findings}
\textbf{Sound; all six theorems check out.} $Z=\sqrt{2\pi/(\lambda t)}$ is the $k=0$ even-moment value
($(2\cdot0-1)!!=0!!=1$, $(\lambda t)^{-1/2}$); the even expectation is the quotient
$(\lambda t)^{-(k+1/2)}/(\lambda t)^{-1/2}=(\lambda t)^{-k}$; the odd expectations vanish by symmetry; and
$\mathrm{Cov}_t[x^2,x]=0$ because $x^2\cdot x=x^3$ and $x$ are both odd powers --- the $\alpha=0$ specialisation
of the primer's $\mathrm{Cov}_t[x^2,x]=-2\alpha/(\lambda^3t^2)+o(t^{-2})$.

\section{Simplifications applied --- and one revealing revert}
\textbf{Landed: a dead \texttt{open Set}.} The file opened \texttt{Real MeasureTheory Set}, but no \texttt{Set}
name is used (the integrals are over all of $\mathbb R$ with \texttt{volume}; there is no \texttt{Ioi} or
interval). Removed; \texttt{Real} (bare $\pi$), \texttt{MeasureTheory} ($\int$/\texttt{volume}), and
\texttt{open scoped Nat} ($!!$) all stay. Rebuild green, no warnings, eight declarations byte-identical.

\textbf{Reverted: a linter-load-bearing dead block.} GPT also flagged --- correctly, in the mathematical sense
--- that \texttt{harmonic\_int\_pow\_odd} contains a change-of-variables detour (\texttt{have this : \dots :=
\dots; clear this}) that is computed and then immediately \texttt{clear}ed, the actual proof going through the
odd-function symmetry \texttt{integral\_neg\_eq\_self}. The block is dead. But removing it made the
\texttt{unusedVariables} linter fire on the theorem's \emph{public} hypotheses \texttt{hlam : 0 < lam} and
\texttt{ht : 0 < t}: the only thing consuming them in that proof was the detour's
\texttt{have hbpos := mul\_pos hlam ht}; the symmetry argument needs no positivity. Because \texttt{hlam} and
\texttt{ht} are part of the frozen public signature (they mirror the even-moment theorem's interface), they
cannot be renamed to \texttt{\_hlam}/\texttt{\_ht} or dropped, and leaving them produces new warnings. So the
``dead'' block is the mechanism that keeps the public hypotheses syntactically used --- it is load-bearing for
the linter. The rebuild caught it (green build, but two new \texttt{unusedVariables} warnings), and the
removal was reverted.

GPT's remaining suggestions (the $x^{2\cdot0}$ rewrite, the \texttt{gibbsCov} function-equality \texttt{show}s,
factoring the duplicated $\sqrt{}/\mathrm{rpow}$ algebra, the universal $(a b c)/(b d)$ rewrite) were declined
as tactic-golf.

\section{Disagreements and open questions}
None. GPT affirmed both the landed removal and the revert.

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{A mathematically-dead block can be load-bearing for the \texttt{unusedVariables} linter under
    a frozen signature.} When a proof carries public hypotheses it does not truly need (here, positivity that
    a symmetry argument ignores), a ``dead'' detour may be the only thing consuming them. Remove it and the
    linter flags the public hypotheses --- which a review tide cannot rename or drop. This is the seabed's
    ``dead code feeds automation'' gotcha in a sharper form: it is not a local that feeds a later tactic, but
    a block that feeds the \emph{linter} on behalf of the interface. The rebuild-plus-linter gate is exactly
    what catches it; ``the build is green'' is necessary but not sufficient --- ``no new warnings'' is the
    real bar.
  \item \textbf{Two dead-code flags in one file can have opposite verdicts.} The dead \texttt{open} was real
    and safe; the dead block was real but unsafe to remove. Triage each against the rebuild independently;
    never batch dead-code removals on the assumption they are all equally safe.
\end{itemize}

\section{Follow-ups}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item If the redundant positivity hypotheses on \texttt{harmonic\_int\_pow\_odd}/\texttt{\_pow\_odd}
    expectations are ever to be cleaned up, that is a forward tide (a deliberate signature change weakening
    the interface), not a review-tide edit.
  \item Remaining tractable 1D never-reviewed files: \texttt{Localisation.lean}, the J-function and
    \texttt{Quartic}/\texttt{Sextic}/\texttt{Monomial} families.
\end{itemize}
