\begin{abstract}
A soundness review of \texttt{Laplace/OneD/HarmonicCovGlobalConstancy.lean}: the closed-form proof
that the perturbed harmonic Gibbs covariance is globally constant,
$\mathrm{Cov}_h(x,x)=1/(\lambda t)$ for all $h$. Result: \textbf{a clean fidelity pass with five
edits} --- a dead \texttt{open Real} and four dead proof-locals. The estimate is correct (the
$h$-dependence cancels exactly), and the one disputed cleanup --- whether \texttt{open Real} is dead
--- was settled by the build against the reviewer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
This is the file the \reviewref[cross-susceptibility capstone]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-oned-harmoniccrosssuscderiv}
now defers to: where the $\kappa_3$ route gave only $\partial_h\mathrm{Cov}_h(x,x)|_0=0$, this file
proves the global statement by direct closed-form evaluation --- square completion
$(\lambda/2)x^2+hx=(\lambda/2)(x+h/\lambda)^2-h^2/(2\lambda)$ turns the perturbed Gibbs into a
translate of the unperturbed Gaussian, and translation-invariance of Lebesgue
(\texttt{integral\_add\_right\_eq\_self}) reduces every perturbed integral to an unperturbed moment.

\section{What was reviewed}
Nine private lemmas (square completion, the shift identity, three integrability witnesses, three
unperturbed-moment closed forms) and four public theorems: the perturbed partition
$Z_h=e^{th^2/2\lambda}\sqrt{2\pi/\lambda t}$, the centroid $\langle x\rangle_h=-h/\lambda$, the
second moment $\langle x^2\rangle_h=1/(\lambda t)+h^2/\lambda^2$, and the headline covariance.

\section{Fidelity / soundness findings}
\textbf{Sound; checked end to end.} The reviewer and I independently confirmed all seven steps: the
square-completion identity; the partition closed form (with the correct $e^{th^2/2\lambda}$
constant); the centroid $-h/\lambda$; the second moment as variance $1/(\lambda t)$ plus shifted-mean
square $(h/\lambda)^2$; the unperturbed $\langle y^2\rangle_0=(1/(\lambda t))Z_0$; and --- the
headline --- $\mathrm{Cov}_h=\langle x^2\rangle_h-\langle x\rangle_h^2
=(1/(\lambda t)+h^2/\lambda^2)-(h/\lambda)^2=1/(\lambda t)$, the $h$-dependence cancelling exactly.
Docstrings accurate, including the ``strengthens the $\kappa_3$ derivative-at-0 result to global''
claim.

\section{Simplifications applied}
\textbf{Five.} A dead \texttt{open Real} (all \texttt{Real.*} qualified; \texttt{open MeasureTheory}
kept for the $34$ bare $\int$, \texttt{open scoped Nat} kept for the double-factorial \texttt{!!} at
the $(2\cdot1-1)\texttt{!!}$ step), and four dead locals: \texttt{hZpos} (a $0<\sqrt{\cdots}$ fact, never
referenced --- and, being a $0<$ rather than $\ne0$ fact, not \texttt{field\_simp} fuel) and
\texttt{hden\_ne} (an $\int\!\cdots\ne0$ fact that goes dead the moment \texttt{rw [hden]} replaces
the denominator with its closed form, after which \texttt{hcommon\_ne} is the witness
\texttt{field\_simp} uses), each in both the centroid and second-moment theorems. Build green at
$8253$ jobs, warning count $0$, signatures byte-identical. GPT affirmed.

\section{Disagreements and open questions}
\textbf{One reviewer finding rejected by the build.} GPT claimed \texttt{open Real} was \emph{not}
dead, on the grounds that \texttt{integrable\_exp\_neg\_mul\_sq} and its two siblings are
unqualified \texttt{Real}-namespace names. But removing \texttt{open Real} builds green --- those
lemmas are root-namespace Mathlib lemmas (reachable without \texttt{open Real}), so the open was
genuinely dead. The build is the arbiter of a namespace claim, and it overruled the reviewer here,
exactly as it has overruled \emph{me} elsewhere (the \texttt{SemiDegenerate} transitive-import case).

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{A \texttt{$0<$} fact is not \texttt{field\_simp} fuel; a \texttt{$\ne0$} fact is ---
    but only while its subject is in the goal.} \texttt{hZpos} ($0<\sqrt{\cdots}$) was never fuel;
    \texttt{hden\_ne} ($\int\ne0$) \emph{was} a $\ne0$ fact, yet went dead once \texttt{rw [hden]}
    rewrote the $\int$ away, leaving \texttt{hcommon\_ne} as the live witness. The discriminator for
    ``is this $\ne0$ local still load-bearing?'' is whether its subject survives the rewrites before
    the \texttt{field\_simp}.
  \item \textbf{The reviewer's namespace guesses are build-checkable, both ways.} GPT was right about
    four dead locals and wrong about \texttt{open Real}; a single \texttt{lake build} adjudicated all
    five. Trusting the reviewer's namespace recall over the green build would have re-introduced dead
    code.
\end{itemize}
