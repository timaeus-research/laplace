\begin{abstract}
The foundation unit for the extended ladder: the fluctuation function $S_\lambda$ is $C^\infty$ in
$a$, the lowering operator $b$ preserves $C^\infty$, and hence the recursive extension
$S_{\mu-k/2}=$\texttt{Sneg}$\,\beta\,\mu\,k$ is $C^\infty$ for base order $\mu>0$. This supplies the
differentiability inputs (\texttt{Differentiable} of \texttt{Sneg} and of its derivative) that the
Weyl commutator \texttt{ladder\_commutator} demands, unblocking the extended raising relation. Zero
\texttt{sorry}/\texttt{axiom}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
The ladder algebra of grammar \S4 needs $C^2$ regularity to apply $[b,b^\dagger]=1$: the commutator
proof differentiates $f$ twice. The extension $S_{\mu-k/2}$ is built by applying the lowering
operator $b=2\beta^{-1/2}\partial_a-\beta^{1/2}a$ repeatedly, so establishing that each stage is
smooth is a prerequisite for the \emph{opposite} (raising) direction. The engine is the derivative
ladder $S'_\lambda=\beta S_{\lambda+1/2}$ (unit~2): the family of fluctuation functions is closed
under $a$-differentiation, so \emph{every} iterated derivative exists and is continuous.

\section{Proof strategy}
Smoothness of $S_\lambda$ is by an auxiliary finite-order induction
\texttt{contDiff\_nat\_fluctuation}: for every $n:\mathbb N$ and $\lambda>0$,
$\texttt{ContDiff}\ \mathbb R\ n\ S_\lambda$. The base is \texttt{contDiff\_zero} (continuity, from
\texttt{hasDerivAt\_fluctuation}); the step uses \texttt{contDiff\_succ\_iff\_deriv} with
$\partial_a S_\lambda=\beta S_{\lambda+1/2}$ staying in the family, so the inductive hypothesis at
$\lambda+1/2$ closes it. Then \texttt{contDiff\_infty} lifts $\forall n$ to $\infty$. Smoothness of
$b f$ is compositional: $\partial_a f$ is $C^\infty$ (\texttt{contDiff\_infty\_iff\_deriv}), and $b f$
is a constant-scaled sum of $\partial_a f$ and $a\,f$. Smoothness of \texttt{Sneg} is then a one-line
induction on $k$ using \texttt{contDiff\_lowerOp}.

\section{Roadblocks and resolutions}
The \texttt{ContDiff} index has migrated to \texttt{WithTop $\mathbb N\infty$}, adding an analytic
grade $\omega=\top$ above $\infty$. Three frictions followed:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{contDiff\_succ\_iff\_deriv} gained a clause $\uparrow n=\omega\to\texttt{AnalyticOn}$.
For finite $n$ this is vacuous, discharged by \texttt{intro h; exact absurd h (by simp)}.
\item Coercions $\mathbb N\to\mathbb N\infty\to\texttt{WithTop }\mathbb N\infty$ fight the finite-$n$
induction; \texttt{Nat.cast\_succ} plus \texttt{exact\_mod\_cast} bridge them.
\item \texttt{.differentiable le\_top} \emph{fails} --- \texttt{le\_top} yields $\le\omega$, not
$\le\infty$, and \texttt{exact\_mod\_cast le\_top} also fails. The working incantation for
``$1\le\infty$'' is simply \texttt{(by norm\_num)}.
\end{itemize}
Also: \texttt{ContDiff.const\_smul} produces $c\bullet x$, not $c*x$; use
\texttt{contDiff\_const.mul} to land the multiplicative form the downstream \texttt{deriv} rewrites
expect.

\section{What was Mathlib, what was new}
Mathlib supplied the \texttt{ContDiff} calculus (\texttt{contDiff\_succ\_iff\_deriv},
\texttt{contDiff\_infty}, \texttt{contDiff\_infty\_iff\_deriv}, \texttt{contDiff\_zero},
\texttt{contDiff\_const}, \texttt{contDiff\_id}). New is the observation that the fluctuation family
is differentiation-closed and the resulting clean induction, plus the smoothness-preservation of the
lowering operator.

\section{Lessons}
On the current pin, \texttt{ContDiff}'s top index is \texttt{WithTop $\mathbb N\infty$}; write finite
regularity through an explicit $\mathbb N$-induction and lift with \texttt{contDiff\_infty}, and reach
for \texttt{(by norm\_num)} rather than \texttt{le\_top} when you need $1\le\infty$ or $2\le\infty$.

\section{Follow-ups}
With \texttt{differentiable\_Sneg} and \texttt{differentiable\_deriv\_Sneg} in hand, the extended
raising relation and number operator become provable (the very next unit).
