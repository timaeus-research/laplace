\begin{abstract}
The anisotropic-scaling tide proved its moment laws over any measure
satisfying a one-parameter pushforward interface, deferring the fact
that Lebesgue measure actually satisfies it. This tide discharges
the deferral: the diagonal map $w \mapsto (c_i w_i)_i$ has
determinant $\prod_i c_i$, so pi-volume scales under the
quasi-homogeneous dilation $\delta_s$ by $s^{-\sum_i q_i}$, and the
weight-recovery theorem becomes unconditional on $\R^{\,\iota}$. The
capstone instantiates the note's own non-separable example:
$x^4 + x^2y^2 + y^4$ is quasi-homogeneous for weights
$(1/4, 1/4)$, so its anisotropic weights are recoverable from
coordinate moment data, end to end on Lebesgue measure. The shape
consult had flagged the determinant step as ``the most brittle piece
of Mathlib determinant plumbing''; it came to five lines
(\code{LinearMap.det\_pi}, \code{det\_smul}, \code{finrank\_self}),
a rare case of the friction estimate being pessimistic.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

Interfaces earn their keep when instances are cheap. The scaling
tide's \code{ScalesMeasure} was designed (by its shape consult) so
that the analytically substantive theorems would not wait on measure
plumbing; the risk of that design is an interface nobody ever
instantiates. This tide closes the loop within the same session: the
concrete instance for the motivating measure, plus the concrete
example the programme had used as its non-separable benchmark since
the degenerate scoping consult rejected it as a \emph{first} step.

\section{The thing formalised}

\code{diagonalMap}~$c$ is the linear map $w \mapsto (c_i w_i)_i$ on
$\iota \to \R$, packaged through \code{LinearMap.pi};
\code{det\_diagonalMap} computes its determinant as $\prod_i c_i$ by
\code{LinearMap.det\_pi} with the one-dimensional factors
$\det(c_i \cdot \mathrm{id}) = c_i$. Since the quasi-homogeneous
dilation is definitionally the diagonal map with entries $s^{q_i}$,
the linear-map Haar theorem\\
(\code{map\_linearMap\_addHaar\_pi\_eq\_smul\_addHaar}) gives\\
\code{scalesMeasure\_qhDilation\_volume}: pi-volume satisfies the
interface with total homogeneity $Q = \sum_i q_i$, the determinant
collapsing to $s^{\sum q_i}$ by \code{rpow\_sum\_of\_pos}. The
recovery corollary
(\code{weights\_eq\_of\_coordSq\_moments\_eq\_volume}) then needs no
scaling hypotheses at all: measurable quasi-homogeneous potentials
on Lebesgue measure, ray-matched coordinate second moments, one
side's moment positivity --- equal weights. Finally\\
\code{mixedQuartic\_quasiHomogeneous} verifies term by term (the
\code{rpow}--\code{npow} bridge per monomial) that
$x^4 + x^2y^2 + y^4$ scales by $s$ under $\delta_s$ with common
weights $1/4$: the example whose integrals do not factor, now inside
the formalised recovery perimeter.

\section{Where progress stalled}

Nowhere. The predicted brittleness of the determinant step did not
materialize: the pin's \code{LinearMap.det\_pi} matches the diagonal
map's packaging exactly, and the bridge from the dilation to the
linear map is a \code{rfl}. The only iteration was instance hygiene
(over-included section variables, flagged by the linter and
tightened per theorem). It is worth recording the pattern: of the
session's consult predictions, the staging calls have all held,
while the friction estimates have erred in both directions --- the
\code{.ofLp} coercion friction was real, the determinant plumbing
was not. Staging advice is the load-bearing part of a consult.

\section{Mathlib lookup versus new mathematics}

All lookup: \code{LinearMap.det\_pi}, \code{det\_smul},
\code{finrank\_self}, the linear-map Haar theorem, and
\code{rpow} algebra. New: nothing mathematical --- this is
infrastructure in the strict sense, the discharge of an interface
obligation. The value is in what it makes unconditional.

\section{What the next tide inherits}

The degenerate programme's formalisable perimeter, per its scoping
consult, is now complete: separable recovery, exact exponent
affinity, abstract scaling, the volume instance, and the concrete
mixed example. What remains beyond --- identification of mixed
coefficients from stratum-aggregated data, the Gelfand--Leray moment
problem, Morse--Bott strata --- is the note's open research
frontier, not tide material. The natural next excursions for the
loop lie elsewhere: the EuclidD transport wrappers if a consumer
wants them, or a fresh direction from the note's remaining
unformalised claims.
