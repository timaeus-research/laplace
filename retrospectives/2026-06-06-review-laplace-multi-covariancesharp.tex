\begin{abstract}
A soundness review of \texttt{Laplace/Multi/CovarianceSharp.lean} (4179 lines): the sharp-rate
multivariate Laplace covariance, \texttt{gibbsCov\_first\_order\_rate\_sharp}, upgrading the weak
$O(t^{-3/2})$ correction to the sharp $O(t^{-2})$ by odd-Gaussian parity vanishing. Result: a clean
fidelity pass --- the parity mechanism that drives the upgrade is correct --- with three stale-status
docstring fixes; the only simplification surface (a large batch of unused \texttt{set} equation-names)
was deferred report-only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
The sharp half of the multivariate covariance result --- the file the
\reviewref[weak-track Covariance review]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-multi-covariance} cross-references.
Where the weak track stops at $O(t^{-3/2})$ via triangle bounds, the sharp track subtracts the
\emph{vanishing} leading half-power correction (odd in $u$, killed against the even Gaussian weight) to
reach $O(t^{-2})$.

\section{What was reviewed}
The headline \texttt{gibbsCov\_first\_order\_rate\_sharp}, the parity-vanishing lemmas
(\texttt{integral\_centered\_bilinear\_cubicJet\_eq\_zero},
\texttt{integral\_dot\_mul\_quadJet\_eq\_zero}, the centering identity
\texttt{integral\_centered\_bilinear\_gaussianWeight\_eq\_zero}), the corrected-bracket rewrite
\texttt{integral\_centered\_bilinear\_eq\_corrected\_bracket} that is the actual weak$\to$sharp upgrade
step, and the sharp intermediate bounds.

\section{Fidelity / soundness findings}
\textbf{Sound; the parity mechanism checks out.} The reviewer confirmed the headline matches the
primer's sharp form --- main term $\langle a,H^{-1}b\rangle$ (not $H$), positive sign, sharp $K/t$ rate
(covariance itself $O(t^{-2})$) --- and, crucially, that \emph{the upgrade from weak to sharp is
genuinely driven by parity}: the two key integrands are odd $\times$ even and vanish, and the
corrected-bracket identity is exactly the place where the weak triangle bound is replaced by
subtracting the vanishing odd piece. ``No wrong rate, no wrong contraction, no sign error.'' (One benign
naming quirk: a parity lemma's jet parameter is named \texttt{q$\varphi$} where the role is
\texttt{q$\psi$}; the statement is correct.)

\section{Simplifications and documentation fixes applied}
\textbf{Three stale-status docstrings.} The sharp headline
\texttt{gibbsCov\_first\_order\_rate\_sharp} still announced ``(\dots, statement only)'' and
``Statement is locked in here; proof is in progress'' --- on the fully-proved headline of the file (the
same false-status pattern the weak headline carried in the previous review). Two helper docstrings
likewise said their work was ``deferred'' / that ``sub-helpers will be added in subsequent stages,''
though the proofs and helpers are present. All three corrected. Comment-only; build green, signatures
byte-identical; the reviewer affirmed.

\textbf{What was deferred.} The file is already minimal on opens (one load-bearing
\texttt{open MeasureTheory}, no dead \texttt{Module}, no redundant re-opens) and has no dead private
lemmas. Its one real dead-code surface is a \emph{large} batch --- roughly thirty unused
\texttt{set \dots with hX} equation-names across eight lemmas. These are build-arbitrated-removable but
low-value and high-count; consistent with the report-only call on the comparable batches in the
\reviewref[IntegralRemainder]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-oned-integralremainder} and
\reviewref[Covariance]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-multi-covariance} reviews, they were left to a
dedicated hygiene tide rather than risk a thirty-name batch edit on a 4179-line file.

\section{Disagreements and open questions}
None. Two pre-existing unused-hypothesis-in-type warnings
(\texttt{abs\_rescaledObservable\_quadratic\_error\_le} here,
\texttt{abs\_rescaledObservable\_linear\_error\_le} in the RescaledIntegrals dependency) are

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{``Proof in progress'' on the headline is now a recurring pattern across the Multi/
    files.} Three consecutive large files (RescaledIntegrals, Covariance, CovarianceSharp) each carried
    a stale ``statement only / in progress'' or ``deferred'' note on work that has since completed ---
    these were written headline-first during development and never refreshed. On a mature multi-stage
    file, the status lines are the highest-yield fidelity target.
  \item \textbf{Know when to defer a large dead-code batch.} A handful of dead locals is worth landing;
    thirty unused equation-names across eight lemmas on a 4k-line file is a hygiene-tide job, not a
    review-tide one --- the value per edit is low and the batch-edit risk is real. Report it precisely
    (names + lemmas) so the sweep is mechanical when it happens.
\end{itemize}
