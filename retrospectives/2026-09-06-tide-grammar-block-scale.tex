\begin{abstract}
For an equal-exponent block $q_0=\dots=q_d=p$ with constant $\xi=a$ the product-density formula of
unit~73 yields two statements needed for the multiplicity-$m$ state-density theorem: a uniform envelope
$c^pZ_{d+1}(c)\le(\prod k_i^{-1})\frac1{d!}E(\beta,L,p,d)\,(1+\log_+(cB))^d$ valid at every scale
$c>0$ and uniformly in $|a|\le L$, and the scale limit
$N^pZ_{d+1}(N\gamma)/\log^dN\to(\prod k_i^{-1})\frac1{d!}\gamma^{-p}A_{p-1}(a)$ for every fixed
$\gamma>0$, by dominated convergence. The tower on any admissible base is also bounded by
$K(1+\log_+x)^m$ at every $x>0$. Zero \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{axiom}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Proof strategy}
Envelope: on $(0,cB]$, $0\le\log(cB)-\log t\le\log_+(cB)+|\log t|\le(1+\log_+(cB))(1+|\log t|)$ and
$e^{-\beta t^2+\beta at}\le e^{\beta L^2/2}e^{-(\beta/2)t^2}$; the integral over $(0,cB]$ is then
bounded by the integral over $(0,\infty)$ of the nonnegative dominating function
(\texttt{setIntegral\_mono\_on}, \texttt{setIntegral\_mono\_set}). Scale limit: after the exact
formula and $(N\gamma)^{-p}=N^{-p}\gamma^{-p}$, the integrand is
$\mathbf 1_{(0,N\gamma B]}\big((\log N+\log(\gamma B)-\log t)/\log N\big)^d t^{p-1}e^{-\beta t^2+\beta at}$,
which converges pointwise to $t^{p-1}e^{-\beta t^2+\beta at}$ and is dominated for $\log N\ge1$ by
$(1+|\log(\gamma B)|+|\log t|)^d t^{p-1}e^{-\beta t^2+\beta at}$, integrable by the binomial expansion
of unit~73. The tower envelope combines \texttt{iterDivPrim\_props} on $(0,1]$ with the complete
expansion \texttt{iterDivPrim\_expansion} on $[1,\infty)$.

\section{Roadblocks and resolutions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{have := hG.ε\_pos} shadows an earlier anonymous \texttt{this}; name the facts.
\item \texttt{positivity} cannot see through \texttt{logPlus} or \texttt{quadKernel}; supply
\texttt{logPlus\_nonneg}/\texttt{quadKernel\_pos} explicitly and use
\texttt{mul\_le\_mul\_of\_nonneg\_*} instead of \texttt{gcongr} when the side goals involve them.
\item Rewriting with \texttt{mul\_assoc} to expose a factor also reassociates the other side; state the
inner inequality with \texttt{suffices} and finish by \texttt{ring}.
\item The final identity with $N^p N^{-p}=1$ closes by \texttt{linear\_combination} against that fact,
after \texttt{set}-folding the integral and the product into atoms.
\end{itemize}
