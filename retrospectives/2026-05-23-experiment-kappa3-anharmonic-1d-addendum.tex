\begin{abstract}
The main retrospective\footnote{\experimentref{2026-05-23-experiment-kappa3-anharmonic-1d}.} reported a non-monotonic
SGLD bias $\Delta_{\rm SGLD-HMC} \in \{+0.092, +0.060, +0.277\}$ across
$\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1.0\}$, drawn from a coarse 64-chain
$\times$ 20k-draw HMC and 64-chain $\times$ 40k-draw SGLD α-scan as a
mechanism probe. At higher precision (64 chains $\times$ 40k draws HMC,
64 chains $\times$ 80k draws SGLD) the non-monotonicity disappears:
$\Delta_{\rm SGLD-HMC}$ scales \emph{monotonically and approximately
linearly} with $\alpha$, slope $\approx 0.22$ at $\alpha \geq 0.5$.
The original non-monotonicity is identified as MC noise.

For Patterning~2 three-point susceptibilities --- which use a third-order
cumulant on a model with intrinsic asymmetry $\alpha$ --- this gives a
practical rule of thumb: \emph{SGLD's pipeline-default ($\varepsilon =
5{\times}10^{-3}$) bias on $\kappa_3$ at WBIC temperature is
$\approx 0.22 \alpha$}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The linearity, in plain prose}

For $\alpha \geq 0.5$, the ratio $\Delta_{\rm SGLD-HMC} / \alpha$ is
flat at $\approx 0.22$ across four cells. For $\alpha \leq 0.25$ the
absolute bias is small relative to MC SE (the bias-to-SE ratio is
$\leq 1.2$), and the apparent $\Delta / \alpha$ value (0.31 and 0.06)
is dominated by noise --- the bias is below the precision floor at
those $\alpha$ values.

\textbf{Conclusion: $\Delta_{\rm SGLD-HMC}(\alpha; t = 100, \varepsilon = 5{\times}10^{-3})
\approx 0.22 \alpha$} at moderate-to-large $\alpha$.

The main run's apparent $\Delta_{\rm SGLD-HMC} = +0.060$ at $\alpha = 0.5$
was 1.5 SE below the trendline ($0.22 \cdot 0.5 = 0.11$); this addendum's
denser run gives $+0.107$, right on the trend. Similarly the main run's
$+0.277$ at $\alpha = 1.0$ was 1.6 SE above; this run gives $+0.223$.
\emph{The "non-monotonic" pattern was MC noise.}

\section{Why this matters}

The Patterning~2 three-point susceptibility framework uses
$\kappa_3$ on a model with intrinsic cubic-asymmetry strength $\alpha$
(the cubic susceptibility of the network's loss landscape). At the
seabed's SGLD pipeline defaults this addendum predicts:
\[
  \hat\kappa_3^{\rm SGLD} \approx \kappa_3^{\rm true} + 0.22 \alpha
  \quad \text{(at $t = 100$, $\varepsilon = 5{\times}10^{-3}$).}
\]
For Patterning~2 to use $\kappa_3$ as a quantitative susceptibility,
either (a) the experiment runs in a regime where $0.22\alpha \ll \kappa_3$
(weak intrinsic skew); (b) the SGLD is run at $\varepsilon \leq 5{\times}10^{-4}$
where the main retrospective's $\varepsilon$-scan showed the bias
vanishing; (c) HMC is used instead.

\section{HMC vs formal at large $\alpha$}

A second observation worth noting: HMC's $t^2 \hat\kappa_3(\alpha = 1.4)
= -1.460$ vs formal asymptote $-1.40$ is $-0.06$ off, a $3.8\sigma$
deviation. At $\alpha = 1.0$ HMC is $+0.015$ above formal ($1.1\sigma$),
also non-trivial. At $\alpha \leq 0.75$ HMC matches within $1\sigma$.

This is consistent with the \emph{finite-$t$ correction} predicted by
the next-order Laplace expansion. The main retrospective's
pre-check showed the correction $+1.25/t$ at $\alpha = 0.5$; the
coefficient scales with $\alpha$ (and possibly higher powers). At
$\alpha = 1.4$, the expected next-order shift is roughly $+1.25 \cdot
(1.4/0.5) / t = +0.035$ if linear-in-α, putting the finite-$t$ value
at $-1.365$ --- but observed is $-1.46$. The deviation is $-0.10$,
suggesting the next-order coefficient grows \emph{super}-linearly in
$\alpha$, or that a higher-order term ($O(\alpha^3 / t)$?) becomes
visible.

This is a candidate for a formalisation tide: extend Tide~9 in
laplace to compute the next-order coefficient
$c_1(\alpha, \lambda, \gamma)$ in $t^2 \kappa_3(t) = -\alpha/\lambda^3
+ c_1 / t + O(1/t^2)$ and check the empirical $\alpha$-scaling.
