\begin{abstract}
Astra~\#12 rank~1. If the random Taylor data $X_n\colon\Omega\to\texttt{CoeffPair}$ converge in
distribution to $Z$, their norms are bounded in probability:
$\forall\eta>0\ \exists M\ \forall^\infty n,\ \mu\{\|X_n\|>M\}\le\eta$
(\texttt{normBounded\_of\_tendstoInDistribution}). The tail $\mu'\{\|Z\|\ge m\}$ tends to $0$ by
continuity from above, and the portmanteau theorem on the closed set $\{\|a\|\ge m\}$ bounds the
limsup of $\mu\{\|X_n\|\ge m\}$ by it. Hence the tightness hypothesis of unit~152 is automatic and
the ordered normalised remainders converge in distribution for any measurable Taylor data
converging in distribution (\texttt{tendstoInDistribution\_normA'}, \texttt{tendstoInDistribution\_normB'}).
Zero \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{axiom}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Mathematics}
Given $\eta\in(0,\infty)$ pick $m\in\mathbb N$ with $\mu'\{\|Z\|\ge m\}<\eta/2$; this exists because
the sets $\{\|Z\|\ge m\}$ decrease to $\emptyset$ and $\mu'$ is finite. The set
$F=\{a:\|a\|\ge m\}$ is closed, so the portmanteau theorem for weak convergence of the laws gives
$\limsup_n\mu\{X_n\in F\}\le\mu'\{Z\in F\}<\eta/2<\eta$, hence eventually $\mu\{\|X_n\|>m\}\le
\mu\{X_n\in F\}<\eta$. As Astra~\#12 stressed, this is norm-boundedness in probability, not
compact tightness (balls in $\ell^1$ are not compact); it is exactly what the ball estimates of
units~149--152 need.

\section{Paper-facing meaning}
The second conclusion of thm:strataempiricalexpansion now holds at chart level for $d=2$ under the
sole probabilistic hypothesis ``the Taylor data converge in distribution in the weighted coefficient
space'': the deterministic bound of unit~150 and the tightness hypothesis of unit~152 are removed.
The paper's separate tightness lemma (appendix prop:convergence~(i)) is thus not needed as an extra
input at this level; it enters only through the (unformalised) functional CLT that supplies the
convergence in distribution itself.
