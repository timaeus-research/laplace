\begin{abstract}
Astra~\#11 rank~2. The Taylor data $(x,y)$ of phase and amplitude are realised as a single point of
Mathlib's Banach space $\ell^1\big((\mathbb N^2)\sqcup(\mathbb N^2),\mathbb R\big)$ after the rescaling
$a(\mathrm{inl}\,k)=x_k\rho^{|k|}$, $a(\mathrm{inr}\,k)=y_k\rho^{|k|}$ (\texttt{CoeffPair},
\texttt{toX}, \texttt{toY}, \texttt{ofPair}, with round trips and $\|a\|=\|x\|_\rho+\|y\|_\rho$).
The norm dominates both weighted norms, so by unit~145 the canonical coefficient maps
$\texttt{coeffA},\texttt{coeffB}\colon\texttt{CoeffPair}\to\mathbb R$ are continuous (locally Lipschitz)
and Borel measurable. This is the domain on which convergence in distribution of the coefficient data
will be stated. Zero \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{axiom}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Mathematics}
The weighted norm $\|x\|_\rho=\sum|x_k|\rho^{|k|}$ is the $\ell^1$ norm of the rescaled array, so
the space of weighted-summable arrays is isometric to $\ell^1(\mathbb N^2)$; a pair of arrays is a
point of $\ell^1$ over the disjoint union of two copies of $\mathbb N^2$, with norm the sum of the
two weighted norms. Working in a single $\ell^1$ space (rather than a product of two) sidesteps the
product-Borel-$\sigma$-algebra question: the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of a product of metric spaces
equals the product $\sigma$-algebra only under second countability, which Mathlib does not provide
for \texttt{lp} in general. Continuity of $A_\alpha,B_\alpha$ follows from the unit-145 Lipschitz
bound on the unit ball around each point (with $M=\|a_0\|+1$ as the common bound) via
\texttt{continuousAt\_of\_locally\_lipschitz}; measurability is then automatic.

\section{Paper-facing meaning}
Astra~\#11 recommended defining the coefficient topology through the arrays and treating the bridge
to the paper's $C^\omega$ topology as an unformalised compatibility statement. The present space is
that definition: a separable Banach space of Taylor data in which the $d=2$ canonical coefficients
are continuous functions, ready for the continuous mapping theorem
(\texttt{TendstoInDistribution.continuous\_comp}) in the next unit.
