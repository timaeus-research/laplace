\begin{abstract}
\noindent The step the H3/H4 shape consult flagged as the hardest of
the multivariate programme: for a $C^2$ loss with vanishing gradient
at the origin, $L(y) - L(0) - \tfrac12\langle y, Hy\rangle = o(\|y\|^2)$
uniformly in direction, and positive-definiteness supplies a
coercivity constant $\lambda\|x\|^2 \le \langle x, Hx\rangle$, giving
the local nondegenerate lower bound
$c\|x\|^2 \le (L(qx) - L(0))/q^2$ on a small ball. The
\texttt{LocalQuadraticApprox} structure packages these fields exactly
as the consult specified for stage H4's dominated convergence, with
derived rescaling-limit and lower-bound theorems and a constructor
from $C^2$ regularity. The predicted difficulty materialized as a
single sharp discovery: Mathlib's mean value inequality has a
`differentiability plus \texttt{fderiv} bound' variant whose
unification against explicit-derivative hypotheses is catastrophic
(a whnf timeout surviving forty times the default heartbeat budget),
while its explicit-derivative sibling elaborates instantly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

Stages H2 (the quadratic Gaussian package) and H3a (the fixed-ray
rescaling limit) are on main. What separates them from stage H4's
dominated convergence is uniformity: the ray limit controls each
direction separately, but the integrable dominator needs a single
lower bound $c\|x\|^2$ valid across the whole rescaled domain. The
shape consult staged this as its own tide and predicted the two
friction points precisely: Mathlib's accessible Taylor remainders are
one-dimensional, and quantitative coercivity from
\texttt{Matrix.PosDef} tempts one into spectral theory.

\section{The thing formalised}

For $L$ of class $C^2$ with $DL(0) = 0$ and $H$ the Hessian matrix at
the origin:
\[
L(y) - L(0) - \tfrac12\langle y, Hy\rangle = o(\|y\|^2), \qquad
\exists\, \lambda > 0:\ \lambda\|x\|^2 \le \langle x, Hx\rangle
\ \ (H \succ 0),
\]
\[
\exists\, \delta, c > 0:\quad 0 < q,\ \|qx\| \le \delta
\ \Longrightarrow\
c\|x\|^2 \le \frac{L(qx) - L(0)}{q^2},
\]
packaged with the rescaling limit in \texttt{LocalQuadraticApprox}.

\section{How the proof works}

\emph{Coercivity} avoids eigenvalues entirely, as the consult ruled:
the quadratic form is continuous and positive on the compact unit
sphere, so it attains a positive minimum $\lambda$, and degree-two
homogeneity extends $\lambda\|x\|^2 \le \langle x, Hx\rangle$ to all
of $\mathbb{R}^d$.

\emph{The Peano remainder} is two applications of the mean value
inequality. First, the gradient linearization: the map
$w \mapsto DL(w) - Hw$ has derivative $D^2L(w) - H$, which continuity
of the second derivative makes $\le \varepsilon$ in operator norm on
a $\delta$-ball; since the map vanishes at the origin, the mean value
inequality gives $\|DL(w) - Hw\| \le \varepsilon\|w\|$ there. Second,
the function $w \mapsto L(w) - \tfrac12\langle w, Hw\rangle$ has
exactly that expression as its derivative (here the symmetry of the
second derivative enters, via Mathlib's
\texttt{second\_derivative\_symmetric}), bounded by
$\varepsilon\|y\|$ on the closed ball of radius $\|y\|$; a second
mean value application gives the $\varepsilon\|y\|^2$ bound.

\emph{The lower bound} takes $\varepsilon = \lambda/4$ of Peano room
below the coercive quadratic, and the rescaling-limit theorem for
package consumers is the same remainder divided by $q^2$, with the
homogeneity identity $\langle qx, H(qx)\rangle = q^2\langle x,
Hx\rangle$ cancelling the scale.

\section{Where it stalled and how it moved}

One genuine stall, resolved by bisection rather than consultation.
The first mean-value application timed out at whnf --- and kept
timing out at $8\times10^6$ heartbeats, minutes of wall clock, even
with every argument made explicit and the space abstracted. Bisection
isolated the bomb to the lemma choice itself:
\texttt{Convex.norm\_image\_sub\_le\_of\_norm\_fderiv\_le} expects
\emph{differentiability} hypotheses and a bound on the
\emph{canonical} \texttt{fderiv}, so handing it
\texttt{HasFDerivWithinAt} facts sends the unifier into an
unbounded definitional unfolding. Its sibling
\texttt{Convex.norm\_image\_sub\_le\_of\_norm\_hasFDerivWithin\_le}
takes the explicit derivative and elaborates in milliseconds. The
symptom (timeout at the theorem's \texttt{:=} line while every
\texttt{have} checks fine) and the fix are now in the repo's error
catalogue. The remaining repairs were routine one-pass items,
including one instructive near-miss: a \texttt{sub\_zero} rewrite ate
the harmless $\|y - 0\|$ occurrence instead of the intended one, the
same positional-rewrite class the catalogue already warns about.

\section{Mathlib lookup versus new mathematics}

Lookup: the compactness extremum, the mean value inequality (correct
variant), second-derivative symmetry, and the bilinear-map derivative
combinators.\footnote{\texttt{IsCompact.exists\_isMinOn},
\texttt{Convex.norm\_image\_sub\_le\_of\_norm\_hasFDerivWithin\_le},
\texttt{second\_derivative\_symmetric},
\texttt{HasFDerivAt.clm\_apply}, \texttt{HasFDerivAt.const\_smul}.}
New (project-level): the sphere-minimum coercivity lemma on
\texttt{qform}, the two-step mean-value derivation of the
multivariate Peano remainder, and the \texttt{LocalQuadraticApprox}
interface with its derived limit and lower-bound theorems.

\section{What this opens}

Stage H4 closes the Hessian-recovery pipeline's analytic half: one
generic dominated-convergence theorem for quadratic-growth
observables against the rescaled Boltzmann factor, specialized to
$1$, $x_i$, $x_i x_j$, with the $(1+\|x\|^2)e^{-c\|x\|^2}$ dominator
supplied by this tide's lower bound and H2's integrability. Then H5
(normalization and covariance bookkeeping) and H6 (pairwise Hessian
recovery), completing the multivariate analogue of
\texttt{base\_recovery}.
