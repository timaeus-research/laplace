\begin{abstract}
This is a 100-line tide that lands the deferred Candidate~B of Tide~12
(the 2D pure-quartic refactor): the affine-bilinear specialisation of
\texttt{gibbsCov}. With the algebra introduced in Tide~I2 (\texttt{gibbsCov\_add}, \texttt{gibbsCov\_smul},
\texttt{gibbsCov\_const}; eleven lemmas) freshly available, the headline
identity
\[
  \mathrm{Cov}_t[a_1\varphi + b_1,\; a_2\psi + b_2]
  \;=\; a_1 \cdot a_2 \cdot \mathrm{Cov}_t[\varphi, \psi]
\]
collapses to a thin composition of one-sided affine reductions. The tide
exists primarily as the first non-trivial \emph{consumer} of the I2
algebra, validating that the algebra is at the right level of abstraction
for downstream multilinearity work. Single session, GPT-5.5~Pro and
Claude voted Candidate~A on the first round, zero-friction proof, 0
\texttt{sorry}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

The 2D pure-quartic tide (1~May 2026) deferred a "Candidate~B": an
affine-bilinear template for the unperturbed Gibbs covariance. Tide~12's
deliberation log records the reasoning: at that point in time, every
covariance bash on $\mathrm{Cov}_t[a_1 x + b_1, a_2 x + b_2]$-shaped
expressions had to unfold \texttt{gibbsExpectation} and rewrite by hand,
because \texttt{gibbsCov} carried no algebra of its own. The right move
was to land the algebra first --- and only \emph{then} land a thin
affine-bilinear corollary as the demonstration that the algebra worked.

The two halves played out as separate tides on 7~May. Tide~I2 added the
eleven \texttt{gibbsCov} algebra lemmas to \texttt{Laplace/Gibbs.lean}
and \texttt{Laplace/Multi/Basic.lean}: symmetry, scalar pulling on each
slot (unconditional), constant-collapse on each slot (unconditional), and
additivity on each slot (conditional on integrability of the four cross
products). With that fresh in cache, the present tide closes Candidate~B
in a single 100-line file that does almost nothing except chain the
algebra together.

The tide is therefore a \emph{narrative-closer} in the strictest sense:
it does not prove a new mathematical fact (the affine-bilinear identity
is content-free given bilinearity); it proves that the I2 abstraction is
load-bearing.

\section{The thing formalised}

\begin{quote}\itshape
For a 1D Gibbs measure $\exp(-t \cdot L(x))\,\mathrm{d}x$ on $\mathbb{R}$
and any pair of observables $\varphi, \psi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$
satisfying the four base integrability conditions on $\exp(-tL)$,
$\varphi\cdot\exp(-tL)$, $\psi\cdot\exp(-tL)$, and
$\varphi\psi\cdot\exp(-tL)$, the Gibbs covariance
$\mathrm{Cov}_t[\varphi, \psi]
   = \langle\varphi\psi\rangle_t - \langle\varphi\rangle_t \langle\psi\rangle_t$
satisfies the affine-bilinear identity
\[
  \mathrm{Cov}_t[a_1 \varphi + b_1,\; a_2 \psi + b_2]
  \;=\; a_1 \cdot a_2 \cdot \mathrm{Cov}_t[\varphi, \psi]
  \qquad (a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2 \in \mathbb{R}).
\]
The constant offsets drop out; the linear scalars factor.
\end{quote}

The Lean signature of the headline\footnote{In
\texttt{Laplace/AffineBilinearCov.lean}.}:

\begin{verbatim}
theorem gibbsCov_affine_affine
    (L : ℝ → ℝ) (t : ℝ) (a₁ b₁ a₂ b₂ : ℝ) (φ ψ : ℝ → ℝ)
    (h_exp : Integrable (fun x => Real.exp (-(t * L x))))
    (h_φ   : Integrable (fun x => φ x * Real.exp (-(t * L x))))
    (h_ψ   : Integrable (fun x => ψ x * Real.exp (-(t * L x))))
    (h_φψ  : Integrable (fun x => φ x * ψ x * Real.exp (-(t * L x)))) :
    gibbsCov L t (fun x => a₁ * φ x + b₁) (fun x => a₂ * ψ x + b₂)
      = a₁ * a₂ * gibbsCov L t φ ψ
\end{verbatim}

Two one-sided helper theorems
(\texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_left}, \texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_right}) take
the same four hypotheses and reduce a single affine slot to a scalar
multiple; the headline is a two-rewrite composition.

\section{Proof strategy}

The whole proof fits in three rewrite chains, one per theorem.

\paragraph{One-sided affine reduction.} For
$\mathrm{Cov}_t[a\varphi + b, \psi]$, split via
\texttt{gibbsCov\_add\_left} into
$\mathrm{Cov}_t[a\varphi, \psi] + \mathrm{Cov}_t[b, \psi]$;
the second summand is zero by \texttt{gibbsCov\_const\_left}; the first
factors as $a \cdot \mathrm{Cov}_t[\varphi, \psi]$ by
\texttt{gibbsCov\_smul\_left}. The four integrability hypotheses
\texttt{gibbsCov\_add\_left} requires (on $a\varphi\cdot\exp$,
$b\cdot\exp$, $a\varphi\psi\cdot\exp$, $b\psi\cdot\exp$) all derive
from the four base hypotheses by \texttt{Integrable.const\_mul} and a
\texttt{simpa [mul\_assoc]} cleanup.

The right-mirror is three lines: \texttt{gibbsCov\_symm} flips the
slots, \texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_left} on the swapped pair runs the same
reduction, \texttt{gibbsCov\_symm} flips back. The only new hypothesis
needed is $\mathrm{Integrable}(\psi\varphi\cdot\exp)$, derived from
$\mathrm{Integrable}(\varphi\psi\cdot\exp)$ by
\texttt{simpa [mul\_comm]}.

\paragraph{Headline.} For the headline,
$\mathrm{Cov}_t[a_1\varphi + b_1, a_2\psi + b_2]$ is reduced by
\texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_left} (with $\psi$ instantiated to the affine
combination $a_2\psi + b_2$) to $a_1 \cdot \mathrm{Cov}_t[\varphi, a_2\psi + b_2]$.
The right slot still carries an affine combination, but
\texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_right} reduces this directly:
$a_2 \cdot \mathrm{Cov}_t[\varphi, \psi]$. Combine with
\texttt{$\leftarrow$ mul\_assoc} to fold $a_1 \cdot (a_2 \cdot \cdot) = a_1 a_2 \cdot \cdot$.

The substantive friction in the headline proof is constructing the
\emph{mixed} integrability witnesses for the first
\texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_left} application, which requires
$\mathrm{Integrable}((a_2\psi + b_2)\cdot\exp)$ and
$\mathrm{Integrable}(\varphi\cdot(a_2\psi + b_2)\cdot\exp)$. Each
expands to a sum of two \texttt{const\_mul} witnesses; this is where
GPT-5.5~Pro's deliberation flag landed (see below).

\section{Roadblocks and resolutions}

Only one substantive friction, and it was anticipated by
GPT-5.5~Pro at deliberation time.

\paragraph{The \texttt{Pi.add}-vs-single-lambda mismatch.}
\texttt{Integrable.add} produces \texttt{Integrable (f + g)} where
\texttt{f + g} is \texttt{Pi.add} (a sum of two functions, not a single
lambda). The first naive attempt to construct
$\mathrm{Integrable}(\varphi\cdot(a_2\psi + b_2)\cdot\exp)$ was

\begin{verbatim}
have := (h_φψ.const_mul a₂).add (h_φ.const_mul b₂)
simpa [add_mul, mul_assoc, mul_add, mul_comm] using this
\end{verbatim}

whose target is the canonical single-lambda integrability of
$\varphi \cdot (a_2 \psi + b_2) \cdot \exp(-tL)$. But
\texttt{simpa} cannot close the gap because the underlying
\texttt{Pi.add} structure of the hypothesis is opaque to the simp set
unless we explicitly normalise the lambda first. This is the failure
mode flagged in the laplace \texttt{CLAUDE.md} from prior tides.

The fix was structural rather than tactical:

\begin{verbatim}
have eq : (fun x => φ x * (a₂ * ψ x + b₂) * exp(-tL)) =
          (fun x => a₂ * (φ x * ψ x * exp(-tL)) + b₂ * (φ x * exp(-tL))) := by
  funext x; ring
rw [eq]
exact (h_φψ.const_mul a₂).add (h_φ.const_mul b₂)
\end{verbatim}

By writing the equality in single-lambda form on both sides
(\texttt{funext + ring}), the goal after \texttt{rw [eq]} is the
canonical \texttt{Pi.add} that
\texttt{(h\_φψ.const\_mul a₂).add (h\_φ.const\_mul b₂)} delivers
verbatim. No \texttt{simpa} cleanup needed.

\paragraph{Deliberation flag.} GPT-5.5~Pro's response identified
exactly this issue at deliberation time: \emph{``if you split an affine
side while the other side is still affine, \texttt{const\_mul} alone is
not quite enough for the first mixed witnesses; you also need
\texttt{Integrable.add} after expanding $(a_1\varphi)(a_2\psi + b_2)\exp$
and $b_1(a_2\psi + b_2)\exp$. After that, the remaining witnesses are
just \texttt{const\_mul} + simp/ring.''} The deliberation also explicitly
recommended exposing one-sided affine helpers \emph{``as reusable building
blocks''}, which is the structure the implementation took.

The voting converged on the first round: both Claude and GPT-5.5~Pro
backed Candidate~A (single affine-bilinear theorem with the four base
integrability hypotheses); Candidate~B (no constants) was deemed
redundant with \texttt{smul\_*}, and Candidate~C (full bilinearity, four
cross terms, sixteen integrability hypotheses) was over-scoped for a
narrative-closer tide.

\section{What was Mathlib, what was new}

\paragraph{Mathlib.} \texttt{Integrable.const\_mul},
\texttt{Integrable.add}, the underlying \texttt{Pi.add} ordering. Standard
ring tactics. No new Mathlib search needed.

\paragraph{New.} Three theorems and zero infrastructure
(\texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_left}, \texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_right},
\texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_affine}). Everything else --- the algebra
itself, the integrability lemmas, the Gibbs definitions --- was already
present from Tide~I2 and the earlier seabed.

The tide stays exactly as small as it should be: 101 lines, three
theorems, no helpers exported beyond what each theorem visibly needs.

\section{Lessons}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{The \texttt{Pi.add}-vs-single-lambda fix is structural,
not tactical.} A \texttt{funext + ring} equality is more reliable than
chained \texttt{simpa} normalisations for bridging the
\texttt{Integrable.add} output to a single-lambda target. Worth promoting
to the laplace \texttt{CLAUDE.md} as the canonical workaround pattern;
the existing entry there flags the symptom but not the cleanest fix.

\item \textbf{One-sided helpers are nearly free when the proof
composes.} The two one-sided affine reductions cost nothing extra
beyond what the headline would have needed inline;
exposing them widens the public surface area without widening the
implementation. GPT-5.5~Pro's deliberation flag here turned out to be
the right architectural call.

\item \textbf{The right time to land a thin consumer is right after the
infrastructure tide.} Tide I2 landed the algebra; this tide consumed it
on a problem that had been deferred specifically for this purpose.
Without I2 the present tide would have been a $\sim$200-line bash;
\emph{with} I2 freshly cache-warm, it was 100 lines and one round of
deliberation. Re-doing the same work \emph{without} the algebra in
place would have meant unfolding \texttt{gibbsExpectation}, rewriting
the products by \texttt{integral\_add} and \texttt{integral\_const\_mul}
hand-rolled, and re-proving constants drop out from scratch --- the
exact pattern that has appeared in three consumer files
(\texttt{OneD/Quartic.lean}, \texttt{TwoD/PureQuartic.lean},
\texttt{TwoD/SemiDegenerate.lean}). The strategic-refactor cleanup on
those files (Candidate~P1 in the v3 survey) is the natural next
narrative beat.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Follow-ups}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{Cleanup of the older affine-cov consumer files.} Three
files in the laplace seabed manually unfold \texttt{gibbsExpectation}
for affine-cov computations that the new \texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_*}
family makes one-line.\footnote{The 1D quartic and the 2D pure-quartic
and semi-degenerate consumer-site bashes; pointers in the v3 survey.}
Estimated $\sim$150 line net deletion, $\sim$200 lines if the cleanup
spans the 2D add-separable file too. Process-flavoured but cost-positive.

\item \textbf{The 2D analogue.} The 2D \texttt{gibbsCov} on
$(\iota \to \mathbb{R})$ has a parallel-mirror algebra in the
multi-index Gibbs file; a multi-index analogue of the affine-bilinear
theorem would take $\sim$30 lines and unblock a parallel cleanup of
the 2D pure-quartic affine-cov bash. Best done in the same tide as
the cleanup if and when that tide is taken.

\item \textbf{Threepoint-side analogue.} The threepoint
\texttt{gibbsCov} (perturbed-Gaussian, with $h \cdot A$ in the
potential) has no algebra of its own yet; an analogous port (call it
``N1'' in the v3 survey) of the I2 algebra would make the C1 / G2
parallel sessions' kappa-3 strict-improvements collapse from
$\sim$50-line bashes to $\sim$5-line specialisations. The two parallel
sessions on C1 / G2 currently in flight will surface whether the port
is needed.

\item \textbf{Promote \texttt{gibbsCov\_affine\_*} to the lemmas section
of \texttt{Laplace/Gibbs.lean}.} If the cleanup tide above lands, the
helpers earn enough consumer sites that they belong with the
\texttt{gibbsCov\_*} algebra rather than in their own file.

\end{itemize}
