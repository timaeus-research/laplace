\begin{abstract}
A soundness review of \texttt{Laplace/OneD/Rescaling.lean}: the rescaling identity
$t\,L(u/\sqrt{\lambda t})=u^2/2+s_t(u)$ for the anharmonic potential, with
$s_t(u)=\tfrac{\alpha}{6\lambda^{3/2}}\tfrac{u^3}{\sqrt t}+\tfrac{\gamma}{24\lambda^2}\tfrac{u^4}{t}$, plus the
coercivity-driven Gaussian-decay bounds and the polynomial bound $s_t(u)^2\le 2(A^2+B^2)(u^6+u^8)/t$. Result:
\textbf{a clean fidelity pass with four dead-code removals} --- a dead \texttt{open Real} and three unused
local hypotheses, all rebuild-arbitrated with the \texttt{unusedVariables} linter as final witness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
This is the rescaling layer of the 1D anharmonic Laplace expansion (primer Stage~2): the substitution
$x=u/\sqrt{\lambda t}$ normalises the harmonic part of $t\,L$ to $u^2/2$ and packages the rest as the
remainder $s_t(u)$, with cubic and quartic coefficients $A=\alpha/(6\lambda^{3/2})$, $B=\gamma/(24\lambda^2)$.
It is the 1D original that the \reviewref[multivariate QuadraticApprox]{2026-06-05-review-laplace-multi-quadratic-approx}
mirrors, and the first review into the non-cumulant 1D layer.

\section{What was reviewed}
Three definitions (\texttt{cubicScale}, \texttt{quarticScale}, \texttt{rescaledPerturbation}), the rescaling
identity and its \texttt{symm} rephrasing, two Gaussian-decay theorems (\texttt{rescaled\_boltzmann\_decay}
and \texttt{rescaled\_max\_decay}), and the polynomial bound \texttt{rescaledPerturbation\_sq\_le} (plus one
private $(a+b)^2\le 2a^2+2b^2$ helper).

\section{Fidelity / soundness findings}
\textbf{Sound; the mathematics is faithful.} The load-bearing check was the rescaling algebra, and it is
correct: with $L=(\lambda/2)x^2+(\alpha/6)x^3+(\gamma/24)x^4$ and $x=u/\sqrt{\lambda t}$, the cubic term gives
$t\cdot\tfrac{\alpha}{6}x^3=\tfrac{\alpha}{6}u^3\tfrac{t}{(\lambda t)^{3/2}}=\tfrac{\alpha}{6\lambda^{3/2}}\tfrac{u^3}{\sqrt t}$
(the key step $t/(\lambda t)^{3/2}=1/(\lambda^{3/2}\sqrt t)$), and the quartic term gives
$\tfrac{\gamma}{24\lambda^2}\tfrac{u^4}{t}$ --- exactly the stated coefficients. The decay bounds correctly
thread coercivity through the rescaling ($c_0=c/\lambda$, then $\min(1/2,c)$), and the polynomial bound has
the right exponents $u^6+u^8$ and the right $t\ge1$ usage $1/t^2\le1/t$.

One benign note, recorded but \textbf{not} escalated and \textbf{not} acted on: the two decay theorems carry a
hypothesis $\gamma>0$ that is already forced by $\lambda>0$ and the discriminant $\alpha^2<3\lambda\gamma$, so
the Lean statements are very slightly stronger than the minimal claim. Removing it is out of a review tide's
scope --- dropping a public hypothesis changes the signature, and besides, the proof genuinely \emph{uses}
$\gamma>0$ to invoke \texttt{anharmonic\_coercive} (whose own signature demands it), so weakening here would
cascade an API change through the dependency. Harmless and left as-is.

\section{Simplifications applied}
\textbf{Four dead-code removals.} A dead \texttt{open Real} (every \texttt{Real.sqrt}/\texttt{Real.exp}/%
\texttt{Real.sq\_sqrt} is fully qualified; the only $\sqrt{}$ symbols are in comments), and three unused local
hypotheses: \texttt{hsqrt\_lamt\_pos} in the rescaling identity (the power computations use
\texttt{Real.sq\_sqrt hlamt.le} directly), and \texttt{hsqrt\_t\_pos}/\texttt{hsqrt\_t\_ne} in the polynomial
bound (\texttt{hsqrt\_t\_sq} comes straight from \texttt{ht\_pos.le}; the surviving \texttt{ht\_ne} is what
\texttt{field\_simp} actually consumes). All four removed in one worktree and \textbf{rebuild-arbitrated}: the
file rebuilt green ($2699$ jobs) with \emph{no} remaining \texttt{unusedVariables} warning and all nine
declarations byte-identical. This last point is the discipline that matters --- the seabed's standing gotcha is
that ``dead'' locals sometimes feed a later tactic implicitly, so a reviewer's unused-flag is only a candidate;
the build plus the linter are the arbiter, and here all four were confirmed genuinely dead.

GPT's six other proposals --- inline the \texttt{field\_simp} nonzero facts, trim the \texttt{nlinarith} hint
list, inline the one-use private helper, inline \texttt{ht\_ne} (which is in fact used), shorten the
\texttt{hLHS} rewrite, and collapse the \texttt{hkey}/\texttt{hgoal} staging --- are tactic-golf or inlining and
were declined.

\section{Disagreements and open questions}
None on the mathematics.

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{A reviewer's ``unused local'' is a candidate, not a verdict.} Three of the four removals here
    were local hypotheses GPT called unused; the honest confirmation was the rebuild with the
    \texttt{unusedVariables} linter clean afterwards, not the reviewer's say-so. (The seabed has a documented
    case where a ``dead'' local was load-bearing for a downstream \texttt{linarith}.) Remove the candidates,
    rebuild, and let the linter's silence be the proof.
  \item \textbf{A redundant hypothesis that the proof actually uses is not removable as a simplification.} The
    $\gamma>0$ on the decay theorems is mathematically implied by the other hypotheses, but it is passed to
    \texttt{anharmonic\_coercive}, so removing it is an API change cascading through a dependency --- a forward
    tide at most, never a review-tide signature-preserving edit.
\end{itemize}
