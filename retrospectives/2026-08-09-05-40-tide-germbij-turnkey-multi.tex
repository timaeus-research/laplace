\section{Setting}

The closing tide of the germbij identifiability arc. After the leading-part
corollary\footnote{\tideref{2026-08-09-05-05-tide-germbij-leading-corollary}.}
the multivariate chain matched the one-dimensional one at every stage except
the last: its final statement still carried three per-$t$ integrability
premises, exactly the ones the 1D turnkey
tide\footnote{\tideref{2026-08-09-04-00-tide-germbij-turnkey}.} had already
discharged on $\mathbb{R}$. That tide's retrospective predicted the
generalisation would be nearly verbatim, since nothing in the three
integrability proofs used the dimension. This tide tested the prediction.

\section{The thing formalised}

Four declarations in \texttt{Laplace/Multi/Turnkey.lean}, sorry-free with
zero warnings. Three integrability lemmas on $\mathbb{R}^d$ (as
$\iota \to \mathbb{R}$ with $\iota$ finite): the minorant
$(L_2 - L_1)^2 \psi\, e^{-t(L_1 + L_2)}$ and each pencil slice are
continuous with support inside $\mathrm{tsupp}\,\psi$, hence integrable;
and the uncurried pencil integrand is integrable on
$[0,1] \times \mathbb{R}^d$ with the restricted product measure, by the
same cylinder-indicator majorant. Then the turnkey
theorem\footnote{\texttt{leading\_part\_pencil\_difference\_lower\_bound'}
in \texttt{Laplace/Multi/Turnkey.lean}.}: for $L_1, L_2 \geq 0$ globally
continuous with $L_2 - L_1 = P + O(\|x\|^{m+1})$ near $0$ for a continuous
positively homogeneous $P$ of degree $m$ not identically zero on the
sphere, with $L_1 + L_2 \leq C_0 \|w\|^2$ on the ball of radius $R$, and
$\psi$ continuous, compactly supported, nonnegative, $\equiv 1$ on that
ball: there exist $\kappa > 0$ and $T_0 > 0$ with
\[
\kappa \cdot t \cdot t^{-m-d/2}
\;\leq\;
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} (L_2 - L_1)\, \psi\,
\bigl( e^{-t L_1} - e^{-t L_2} \bigr)\, dw
\qquad \text{for all } t \geq T_0 .
\]
Every hypothesis is a mathematical property of the potentials, the leading
part, and the bump; no integrability premise survives. This is the
multivariate germbij Theorem~7.3 lower bound in its final form, and with
it both chains of the arc, one-dimensional and multivariate, terminate in
statements of the same character.

\section{Proof strategy}

A parametrised copy of the 1D turnkey file. The two pointwise lemmas are
the compact-support integrability lemma\footnote{%
\texttt{Continuous.integrable\_of\_hasCompactSupport}.} with support
transported from $\psi$; the product lemma is the compact-cylinder bound,
indicator majorant, product-restriction rewrite, and domination, with
$[0,1] \times \mathrm{tsupp}\,\psi$ compact because $\mathbb{R}^d$ is a
proper space (which Lean supplies silently through the same instances as
on $\mathbb{R}$). The turnkey theorem destructures the leading-part
corollary and feeds it the three lemmas. No consult: the 1D consult's
blueprint was already recorded and its idioms carried over unchanged.

\section{Roadblocks and resolutions}

None. The file compiled on the first \texttt{lake build}, the first
first-attempt-clean tide of the arc. Both elaboration snags the 1D tide
hit (implicit endpoints of \texttt{isCompact\_Icc} unifying with the
ambient $t$; an anonymous constructor inside a \texttt{rw} argument) were
avoided by inheriting their fixes from the 1D source rather than
rediscovering them.

\section{What was Mathlib, what was new}

All Mathlib, at one remove: the tide's real input was the previous tide's
file, and the Mathlib content (compact-support integrability, the
product-restriction identity, indicator domination) came through it
unchanged. New: only the observation, now verified, that the 1D
integrability layer was dimension-free.

\section{Lessons}

When a retrospective predicts a generalisation is mechanical, the
prediction is testable and worth testing immediately: the marginal cost
here was one file and one build. Copying a proven file and specialising
beats re-deriving from the consult blueprint; the second author of a
mirror-shaped tide is the previous tide, not Mathlib.
