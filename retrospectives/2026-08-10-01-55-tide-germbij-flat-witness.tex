\section{Setting}

The flat-invisibility tide proved that \emph{any} flat perturbation
hides from all expansion orders, but the theorem quantifies over a
hypothesis package: continuous, bounded, nonnegative, flat. The
germbij note's Proposition~4.1 discussion is anchored on a specific
function --- $e^{-1/x^2}$, the textbook example of flatness --- and
until a concrete instance satisfies the package in Lean, the
formalised counterexample is conditional. This tide closes that gap.
It was deferred from the parent tide by joint vote, with the proof
route already supplied in the parent consult, so it is the cheapest
kind of tide: pure instantiation, stacked on the parent branch, with
the deliberation inherited.

\section{The thing formalised}

Eight declarations in \texttt{Laplace/OneD/FlatWitness.lean},
sorry-free with zero warnings. The definition extends $e^{-1/x^2}$ by
$0$ at the origin; the load-bearing lemma is the \emph{global}
factorial bound\footnote{\texttt{flatWitness\_le\_factorial\_mul\_pow}.}
\[
e^{-1/x^2} \;\le\; n!\,x^{2n}
\qquad\text{for all } x \text{ and all } n,
\]
from which nonnegativity, the bound by $1$, continuity, and the
flatness package all follow. The two instantiation
corollaries\footnote{\texttt{flat\_witness\_invisible} and
\texttt{flat\_witness\_superpolynomial}.} feed the witness to the
parent theorems: perturbing the harmonic potential by $e^{-1/x^2}$
shifts every compactly-supported-observable integral by $O(t^{-N})$
for every $N$, i.e.\ superpolynomially. Proposition~4.1's
counterexample is now witnessed, not just quantified.

\section{Proof strategy}

Everything reduces to one inequality. Setting $s = 1/x^2$, the single
term of the exponential series gives $s^n/n! \le e^s$
(\texttt{Real.sum\_le\_exp\_of\_nonneg} plus
\texttt{Finset.single\_le\_sum}), and rearranging yields
$e^{-s} \le n!/s^n = n!\,x^{2n}$; in Lean the rearrangement goes
through the product identity $x^{2n} s^n = 1$ rather than nested
divisions. Continuity at the origin is a squeeze against the $n = 1$
case ($0 \le f \le x^2$); away from the origin it is composition. The
numerical check (executed before formalisation) confirmed the bound
on a grid, with the maximum ratio $0.3679$ at $n = 1$ matching the
analytic supremum $e^{-n}n^n/n!$ exactly.

\section{Roadblocks and resolutions}

Minor, all known classes. The catalogued cascading-rewrite gotcha
struck again: rewriting $1 = e^0$ to compare $e^{-1/x^2} \le 1$ also
rewrote the $1$ inside $1/x^2$, leaving \texttt{linarith} facing an
opaque $e^0/x^2$ atom; the fix is a \texttt{calc} through
$e^0$ that never rewrites the goal. \texttt{continuousAt\_pow} takes
its arguments as $(x, n)$, not $(n, x)$. One dead tactic after
\texttt{field\_simp}. Three fix cycles total, all catalogue lookups.

The genuine friction was infrastructure: \texttt{seed\_worktree}
failed on this host because the macOS system bash (3.2) has no
associative arrays, so every worktree this session was silently
falling back to \texttt{lake exe cache get}. The worktree was seeded
manually by hardlinking from the parent tide's tree
(\texttt{rsync --link-dest}), and the script was fixed portably in
SRI (an \texttt{awk} lookup over manifest lines) and smoke-tested:
ten packages, Mathlib included, now hardlink correctly.

\section{The deliberation}

No fresh consult. The parent tide's consult recommended exactly this
follow-up and supplied the route:
\begin{quote}
For $f(x) = e^{-1/x^2}$ (extended by $0$) one can in fact obtain the
flat bound globally: $e^{-1/x^2} \le n!\,x^{2n}$. [\dots] Thus one may
take $C = n!$ and any positive $\delta$.
\end{quote}
Its advice to verify \texttt{Real.sum\_le\_exp\_of\_nonneg} rather
than trust a guessed name was followed and correct: the lemma exists,
and the guessed direct form (\texttt{pow\_div\_factorial\_le\_exp})
does not. This is the documented proceed-without-new-consult path:
prior deliberation on this exact candidate plus an executed numerical
check.

\section{Mathlib vs new}

Mathlib lookup: the exponential series bound, single-term extraction
from a finite sum, the squeeze lemma, continuity combinators, and the
eventually-nonzero neighborhood filter. New: only the assembly. Notably,
Mathlib's own \texttt{expNegInvGlue} ($e^{-1/x}$ for $x > 0$) was not
needed --- the even variant is self-contained in 140 lines.

\section{What the next tide inherits}
